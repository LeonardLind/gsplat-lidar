# aerial-ground-fusion

## Summary

BOTTOM LINE: For this project, merging drone (aerial) images and Insta360 ground images into ONE splat (option b) costs a lot and gains little. Use it only as an optional experiment. The evidence-based choice: (a) LiDAR + Insta360 for the first test, then (c) the drone as a separate aerial layer registered into the shared (LiDAR) frame as a later optional phase. Nothing found argues for bringing the drone forward.

1) WHAT THE LITERATURE SHOWS. Most aerial+ground radiance-field papers are urban, often synthetic, and run on A100-class GPUs.
- MatrixCity (ICCV 2023): naive aerial+street fusion degraded NeRFs badly (Mip-NeRF360 aerial PSNR 28.37 -> 17.07; TensoRF 27.26 -> 21.44).
- Horizon-GS (CVPR 2025, closest state of the art): vanilla 3DGS joint training has "conflicting gradient accumulation". Street views prune occluded Gaussians, aerial views regrow them, and joint training "consistently underperformed separate training". Their fix is two-stage training (aerial gradients first, then street-only densification), camera-sampling balancing (2:1 then 1:1) and multi-LOD. It gives big aerial gains (30.59 vs 28.44 dB vs Scaffold-GS). Street views only reach parity (23.80 vs 23.84 dB). So even the best unified method does not make ground views better than a strong ground-capable baseline; the benefit is one model that renders well from both heights. Real data needed bridging "transitional views", ContextCapture poses, an A100 80 GB and ~4 h/scene. Code is released but under the Inria 3DGS non-commercial license.
- UC-GS (BMVC 2024): with uncertainty-weighted aerial pixels, road views gain only +0.41 to +0.68 dB, on synthetic AirSim/UE data with perfect poses. Naive A+G mainly helped slightly shifted or tilted views.
- Practitioners (heliguy/XGRIDS LCC Aerial-Ground Map Fusion, DJI Terra guides): aerial gives roofs, context and site framework, ground capture gives eye-level detail. Commercial fusion relies on RTK on BOTH devices plus extra ascending bridging shots; others just composite separate splats in SuperSplat. The BLK2GO has no GNSS, so the RTK route is unavailable without GCPs.

2) KNOWN PROBLEMS.
- SfM matching: SIFT degrades past ~30 deg of out-of-plane viewpoint change and keeps >50% accuracy only to ~50 deg on planar scenes. Nadir-vs-horizontal is ~90 deg. DRAGON measured COLMAP registering only 50% of mixed drone+ground images. Learned matchers help in urban scenes: AerialMegaDepth fine-tuning lifts DUSt3R RRA@5 from 5.2% to 56%. Sky2Ground (CVPR 2026) and Aug-2026 detector-free aerial-ground SfM still report hard failures. MASt3R is CC BY-NC-SA; RoMa (MIT) and LightGlue (Apache) are permissive.
- Multi-scale aliasing: needs Mip-style antialiasing (gsplat antialiased=True) or LoD (Octree-GS).
- Appearance/lighting: use per-camera/per-frame compensation. PPISP is in gsplat, Apache, and builds per-camera modules from COLMAP camera ids. Bilateral grid and app_opt are also available.
- Densification imbalance: Horizon-GS-style sampling is not in stock gsplat, so custom code is needed.
- Fixed Gaussian budget on 16 GB: adding aerial content takes Gaussians away from ground detail. gsplat MCMC is 1M = 1.98 GB, 3M = 4.99 GB on Mip-NeRF360. VRAM itself is not the bottleneck at 10x10 m.

3) FOREST-SPECIFIC REASONS MERGING IS WEAK.
- Above-canopy drone images see crown TOPS; ground 360 images see trunks, understory and crown UNDERSIDES against bright sky. Co-visible surfaces are few (edges, gaps), so cross-view matching has little to work with, whatever the matcher.
- In tropical rainforest, gaps are "rare or absent" and SfM usually cannot map the ground (Swinfield 2019). In boreal forest, photogrammetric DEMs are reliable only in open or sparse areas (Brown 2024: RMSE <1.5 m overall, 0.31 m over open terrain).
- Merging also creates an opacity conflict at the canopy layer. Aerial views want opaque canopy-top Gaussians; ground views looking up through gaps want them transparent. This is the Horizon-GS gradient conflict in its worst form. (This is my inference, not a measured result.)
- Moving foliage (wind, rotor downwash) breaks the static-scene assumption most in the canopy. The canopy is also exactly where the drone adds information.
- Resolution: a C0 Mini-class drone (48 MP, 82 deg FOV) at 30 m above the canopy gives ~5 mm/px on crown tops. Insta360 8K equirect gives ~1.6 mm/px at 2 m but ~12 mm/px at 15 m (canopy undersides). The drone therefore resolves the canopy better, but a ground-walk viewer never sees that side.

4) REGISTRATION. With LiDAR present, the shared frame should stay the LiDAR frame.
- Drone imagery would be solved in its own SfM (nadir+oblique grid with EXIF/RTK GNSS priors; COLMAP pose-prior mapper 3.11+) and registered to the BLK2GO cloud. Options: GCP targets in gaps/edges visible from above and in the LiDAR, plus ICP/canopy-shape registration on terrain, trunks at edges and crowns.
- Literature accuracy for aerial-to-ground forest cloud registration is decimetre level: 0.24 m average canopy-shape TLS-ULS; 0.2-0.5 m typical tree-based; 0.05 m northern / 0.17 m tropical for UAV-LiDAR-to-MLS in high-density canopy. Photogrammetric clouds will be worse.
- That error is fine for a separate layer (c) but too coarse for a merged splat (b), which needs roughly pixel-consistent (cm-level) poses near shared surfaces. Option (b) therefore also needs bridging capture plus joint bundle adjustment, or gsplat pose_opt for small residuals.
- When no LiDAR is present, drone GNSS (RTK preferred) defines a georeferenced metric frame. The drone could then be the scale/georef provider for a splat-only project. That is the one scenario where a joint SfM (b-style) has a distinct benefit (inference).

5) VERDICT.
- 10x10 m Swedish plot: (b) is not worth it. Expected ground-view gain ~0 dB, with a real risk of degradation, large SfM/training engineering effort, and NC-licensed reference code. (c) adds canopy-top, terrain context and surroundings for overview and fly-up views at modest cost and with no risk to the ground splat.
- Larger tropical sites: (c) is the only practical option. A closed canopy prevents bridging and the drone sees essentially only the canopy. LoD/chunked aerial training (Octree-GS / CityGaussian class) becomes relevant beyond a few hectares.
- Search budget ran out near the end of this research. Real-data aerial+ground forest splat comparisons and current drone price and regulation details were not searched further.

## Recommendations

- **Phase 1: keep option (a), LiDAR + Insta360 only. Do not buy or fly a drone for the first 10x10 m test.** — There is no evidence that aerial images improve ground-level views of a forest splat. Horizon-GS only reaches street-view parity, UC-GS gives +0.4-0.7 dB on synthetic data, and naive fusion degrades results. The viewer's main mode is ground navigation, which never sees crown tops. Adding a drone multiplies the SfM, registration and training failure modes before the core LiDAR-splat alignment is proven. _Alternatives:_ If an overview image is wanted for the test, a handful of drone photos can be used as documentation only, without integration.
- **Later optional phase: implement option (c), the drone as a separate aerial model. Build a drone-only SfM (nadir grid + 4-direction obliques with EXIF/RTK GNSS priors, COLMAP pose-prior mapper) and a drone-only gsplat splat (antialiased=True, PPISP). Register it into the shared frame, which is the LiDAR frame when present. The viewer shows it as its own layer with a camera-height LoD switch or cross-fade at canopy height. Partition the volume using the LiDAR canopy height model, so the ground splat owns everything below the canopy and the aerial splat owns above-canopy surfaces plus surroundings beyond the plot.** — It adds canopy-top, terrain and context coverage at modest cost (estimate: days of engineering, 20-30 min of field time). It cannot degrade the ground splat. Decimetre-level registration (0.05-0.25 m in the literature) is acceptable for a separate layer. It also fits the modular source architecture: the drone module emits images, poses, a GNSS frame and a splat layer. _Alternatives:_ Use a drone photogrammetric mesh or DSM instead of a splat for the aerial layer (cheaper to stream, LuciadRIA-friendly).
- **Treat option (b), one merged splat, as a research experiment only. Attempt it only at an open Swedish site with a forest edge or clearing, with bridging capture and a seamless ground-to-sky fly-up as a hard requirement. Never attempt it in closed tropical canopy.** — It needs cm-consistent joint poses, which in turn needs shared co-visible surfaces; a forest has few. It also needs custom trainer changes (camera-group sampling, stage-wise densification) and fights an opacity conflict at the canopy. The reference implementations are licensed non-commercial. _Alternatives:_ A 'loose merge': train one gsplat model on ground + drone images, using the poses from (c)'s registration plus gsplat pose_opt. It is cheaper but will likely show ghosting at crown edges and gaps.
- **If (b) is attempted, follow this technique: (1) Bridging capture: a descending drone orbit at a gap or edge (e.g. 45-60 deg oblique, stepping down in altitude), ascending shots at plot corners (heliguy used 1 m steps), and Insta360 on a 3-5 m pole. (2) Joint SfM with pose priors and learned matching: ALIKED/LightGlue in COLMAP 4.x or RoMa (MIT). MASt3R or aerial-MASt3R are CC BY-NC-SA and suitable for research only. (3) Separate COLMAP camera ids per sensor, so gsplat PPISP and appearance embeddings separate the drone and Insta360 ISPs. (4) Horizon-GS-style training reimplemented in gsplat: stage 1 aerial-weighted sampling (2:1) with aerial-only densification stats, stage 2 ground-only densification at 1:1. (5) antialiased=True, an MCMC cap of ~3-5M on 16 GB, images downscaled to ~1600 px. (6) Sky masks on ground and oblique images, and operator masks. (7) Gather densification stats from the LiDAR-occupied volume only, or use a LiDAR depth prior.** — These are the components that the evidence (Horizon-GS ablations, PPISP, Mip-Splatting/gsplat antialiasing, Pix4D/XGRIDS bridging practice) shows are necessary. They are reimplemented on Apache-licensed code to avoid NC-license exposure. _Alternatives:_ Use Horizon-GS code directly, for internal evaluation only (Inria NC license).
- **Drone capture protocol, valid for either (b) or (c). Fly the same day as the ground walk, within about ±30 min. Prefer overcast or stable light and low wind. Lock exposure and white balance on both the drone and the Insta360. Shoot 48 MP stills rather than video, using hover-and-shoot or slow speed if the shutter is electronic. Fly a nadir grid plus 4-direction obliques at 30-45 deg off-nadir with ≥80% front and ≥70% side overlap, about 20-30 m above the canopy top. Cover a buffer well beyond the 10x10 m plot (e.g. ~60x60 m) so SfM is well conditioned. Place 3-5 GCP targets in gaps or at edges that are visible from the air, in the BLK2GO cloud and in the Insta360 frames.** — Overlap and oblique values come from Pix4D and DJI-workflow guides. The boreal study found overcast best for sparse forest. Foliage motion and lighting changes are the main sources of cross-session inconsistency. GCPs are the only robust bridge because the BLK2GO has no GNSS. _Alternatives:_ An RTK drone, either without GCPs (absolute georeferencing) or with them as a check. Inside the plot the LiDAR frame still governs.
- **Module design implication: the drone source module should output its own pose set, a GNSS/ENU frame and a splat/mesh layer, plus a registration transform (with an uncertainty estimate) to the shared frame. Joint training with ground images should be an explicit opt-in 'merge' flag, not the default. With no LiDAR, use drone GNSS (RTK preferred) as the georeferenced metric frame. In a drone + Insta360-only project, a joint SfM is the main way the ground splat obtains metric scale.** — This keeps (c) as the default path and still allows (b) experiments. It also covers the source-combination matrix the user asked for, including drone-only and drone+splat without LiDAR. _Alternatives:_ Scale the drone + Insta360 case with a measured scale bar or GCPs instead of a joint SfM.

## Load-bearing findings (claim + sources)

- [high] Naively training one radiance field on aerial + street images degrades quality; this is well documented. — https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16553 https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745
- [high] Even the best unified aerial-to-ground method (Horizon-GS, CVPR 2025) does not improve street-level views over a strong baseline. It mainly improves aerial views and lets one model serve both heights. — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Jiang_Horizon-GS_Unified_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_for_Large-Scale_Aerial-to-Ground_Scenes_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf
- [medium] Where aerial images were shown to improve ground views, the gain was small (+0.4 to +0.7 dB). It came on synthetic urban data with perfect poses and required a specialised training method. — https://sainingzhang.github.io/project/uc-gs/ https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2408.15242 https://github.com/SainingZhang/UC-GS
- [high] Standard COLMAP/SIFT cannot reliably match nadir drone images to ground-level images (~90 deg viewpoint gap). Bridging images or specialised learned matchers are required, and the matchers are still unreliable. — https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~lowe/papers/ijcv04.pdf https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6359040 https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01761
- [high] Practitioners merging drone and ground data rely on RTK/GCPs and explicit bridging shots rather than pure image matching. Many simply composite separately trained splats. — https://www.heliguy.com/blogs/posts/drone-ground-map-fusion-3dgs/ https://www.heliguy.com/blogs/posts/drones-for-gaussian-splatting/ https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/203465895-merge-nadir-image-with-45-image-
- [high] In forests, a drone above the canopy sees mostly crown tops, terrain only in gaps, and essentially no understory. In dense tropical forest it sees almost no ground. — https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/11/8/928 https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/avsc.12797 https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316588344_Accuracy_of_Photogrammetric_UAV-Based_Point_Clouds_under_Conditions_of_Partially-Open_Forest_Canopy
- [medium] In a forest, aerial and ground cameras see mostly different surfaces (crown tops vs. trunks and crown undersides). This weakens both cross-view matching and the benefit of merging, and it creates an opacity conflict at the canopy layer. — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745 https://realhorizons.ai/blog/outdoor-gaussian-splatting-capture-guide/
- [medium] Registering an above-canopy drone model to a ground/handheld LiDAR cloud without shared GNSS reaches decimetre accuracy. That is enough for a separate layer but not for one merged splat. — https://doi.org/10.3390/f16081347 https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17203403 https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09896
- [high] gsplat (Apache-2.0) already has most of what a careful merge needs: Mip-style antialiasing, per-camera/per-frame appearance (PPISP), bilateral grid, per-image appearance embeddings, pose refinement, MCMC with a hard Gaussian cap, and packed/sparse-grad memory modes. It lacks camera-group sampling weights and stage-wise densification. — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat/main/examples/simple_trainer.py https://docs.gsplat.studio/main/apis/rasterization.html https://docs.gsplat.studio/main/tests/eval.html
- [high] The dedicated aerial-ground and LoD trainers (Horizon-GS, Octree-GS, UC-GS-base Scaffold-GS, Hierarchical-3DGS) are under the Inria 3DGS non-commercial license or have no clear license. They are usable for evaluation, not as production code for a Hexagon tool. — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRobotLab/HorizonGS/master/README.md https://github.com/city-super/Octree-GS https://raw.githubusercontent.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting/main/LICENSE.md

## Key numbers

- Horizon-GS street-view PSNR vs Scaffold-GS (small scenes): 23.80 vs 23.84 dB (3D-GS 21.81) (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745)
- Horizon-GS aerial-view PSNR/LPIPS vs Scaffold-GS: 30.59/0.094 vs 28.44/0.191 (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745)
- Horizon-GS ablation, removing camera balancing: -0.74 dB (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745)
- Horizon-GS compute: A100 80 GB, ~4 h per large scene, images resized to 1600 px (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01745)
- MatrixCity naive aerial+street fusion (Mip-NeRF360, aerial PSNR): 28.37 -> 17.07 dB (TensoRF 27.26 -> 21.44) (https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16553)
- UC-GS road-view gain from uncertainty-weighted aerial images (synthetic): +0.68 dB NYC, +0.41 dB SF (https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2408.15242)
- COLMAP registration of mixed drone+ground views (DRAGON baseline) vs DRAGON: 50.00% vs 97.47% (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01761)
- DUSt3R aerial-ground RRA@5, off-the-shelf vs fine-tuned on AerialMegaDepth: 5.20% -> 55.96% (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13157)
- SIFT matching accuracy limit: >50% up to ~50 deg viewpoint change (planar); local features fail beyond 25-30 deg out-of-plane (https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~lowe/papers/ijcv04.pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6359040)
- UAV-to-ground forest point cloud registration accuracy: 0.24 m avg (canopy shape, TLS-ULS); 0.2-0.5 m typical; 0.17 m tropical / 0.05 m northern (ULS-MLS) (https://doi.org/10.3390/f16081347 ; https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17203403)
- Boreal UAV-SfM DEM accuracy: RMSE <1.5 m in all DEMs; 0.31 m over open terrain (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/avsc.12797)
- gsplat MCMC training memory (Mip-NeRF360, A100): 1M Gaussians 1.98 GB; 3M 4.99 GB; default strategy 5.62 GB (https://docs.gsplat.studio/main/tests/eval.html)
- Overlap recommendations for drone splat/photogrammetry: Nadir 80-85% front / 70-80% side; oblique ≥85%, two rounds (https://www.thefuture3d.com/blog/gaussian-splatting-drone-data-dji-m4e-workflow/ ; https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/203465895-merge-nadir-image-with-45-image-)
- Commercial aerial-ground fusion example (XGRIDS + M4E): 30 m ortho + 70 m oblique cross-hatch; 1 m ascending corner shots; RTK on both; 5 h processing on RTX 5090/128 GB (https://www.heliguy.com/blogs/posts/drone-ground-map-fusion-3dgs/)
- Drone GSD at 30 m range, C0 Mini 4 Pro (calculated): ~5.2 mm/px at 48 MP; ~10.4 mm/px at 12 MP (https://www.dji.com/global/mini-4-pro/specs (calculation))
- Insta360 8K equirect angular resolution (calculated, assumes 7680 px width): ~0.82 mrad/px: 1.6 mm at 2 m, ~12 mm at 15 m (background knowledge + calculation)
- Forest LiDAR-guided UAV-image GS quality: PSNR 24.94, SSIM 0.773, LPIPS 0.231; DBH RMSE 2.705 cm (https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18111696)

## risks

- Cross-view SfM failure: drone and Insta360 images form disconnected components. In closed canopy this is near-certain, which forces registration through LiDAR, GCPs or GNSS at decimetre accuracy.
- Opacity and densification conflict at the canopy layer in a merged splat. Ground views looking up through gaps fight aerial canopy-top Gaussians, giving floaters, holes or haze at crown level.
- Under a fixed 16 GB Gaussian budget, merging takes Gaussians away from ground-level detail, which is the viewer's main use case.
- Foliage motion from wind and rotor downwash between and during flights breaks the static-scene assumption, mostly in the canopy.
- Lighting and appearance mismatch between drone and Insta360 sessions (sun angle, sensor ISP). Mitigated but not eliminated by PPISP or bilateral grids.
- License exposure: Horizon-GS, Octree-GS, Hierarchical-3DGS and MASt3R are non-commercial. Using them beyond evaluation inside Hexagon needs legal review.
- Registration error of 0.05-0.5 m for drone-to-LiDAR forest alignment shows as visible seams or ghosting if layers are shown simultaneously near the canopy.
- The evidence base is mostly urban and synthetic. No peer-reviewed quantitative study of a merged aerial + ground-level splat in forest was found, so expected gains are inferred.
- Operational: flying a drone near or under the canopy risks collisions and GNSS loss. Permits and operator registration add lead time (regulatory specifics not verified here).
- Web search budget ran out late in this research. Some follow-ups (real-data aerial-ground forest splats, drone pricing, Swedish dissemination-permit status) were not searched.

## open_questions

- Is there any real-data study of a merged aerial + ground-level splat in forest, with quantitative ground-view metrics? None was found before the search budget ran out.
- How well does UAV photogrammetry register to a BLK2GO cloud specifically, given that the BLK2GO sees crowns from below and has ~25 m range? Expect it to be worse than the ULS-MLS figures (0.05-0.17 m).
- Does stitched 360 drone video (e.g. Antigravity A1) reconstruct better than a standard drone camera for bridging and appearance consistency with the Insta360?
- Exact current status of Swedish aerial-imagery dissemination rules (Lantmäteriet) and the EU class-mark requirements for the candidate drone. These belong to the regulations research topic.
- Whether the web viewer (Spark/Three.js or LuciadRIA) can depth-sort two splat layers together for a seamless fly-up, or needs a hard LoD switch.
- Whether gsplat's antialiased mode (2D filter only) is enough for ~3-10x scale differences, or a 3D smoothing filter (Mip-Splatting) or LoD is needed.

## questions_for_user

- Must the viewer support flying above the canopy (bird's-eye or fly-up), or is it primarily a ground-level walkthrough? This decides whether a drone adds any visible value.
- Do you have or plan an RTK drone, or GCP targets that can be scanned by the BLK2GO and seen from the air?
- Will test sites include a clearing, road or forest edge where drone and ground cameras can see the same surfaces (needed for any merged-splat attempt)?
- Is the pipeline for internal R&D only, or could it ship commercially? This determines whether non-commercial reference code (Horizon-GS, Octree-GS, MASt3R) may be used.
- Is a drone-only or drone + Insta360 (no LiDAR) mode a real use case, or mainly for completeness? It changes whether the drone must provide the metric or georeferenced frame.

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The recommendations hold up. Independent checks support (a) for Phase 1, (c) as the later optional drone phase, and (b) as research-only, and none of the evidence argues for bringing the drone forward. Every paper number I could re-check matched: Horizon-GS Tables 1 and 2, UC-GS, AerialMegaDepth, Sky2Ground, the 2608.15251 paper, the gsplat memory figures, and the registration RMSEs. Several corrections make the case against (b) stronger:
(1) The Horizon-GS ablations the report wants to port (camera balancing, densification balancing, LOD) change STREET PSNR by only -0.04, -0.06 and -0.01 dB. All their benefit is in aerial views. On large scenes, Hierarchical-GS matches or beats Horizon-GS at street level on 2 of 3 scenes.
(2) The naive A+G result in UC-GS is +0.02 dB (NYC) and -0.18 dB (SF).
(3) The DRAGON, MatrixCity, UC-GS and AerialMegaDepth evidence is synthetic or semi-synthetic.
(4) The commercial fusion vendor recommends at least an RTX 3090/4090 and 64 GB RAM, above the user's laptop.

Changes I would make:
(a) Add a regulatory gate to the drone phase. In Sweden, publishing or sharing aerial imagery needs a Lantmäteriet dissemination permit, which very likely covers a drone-derived layer in a web viewer. Transportstyrelsen operator registration is needed even for a C0 camera drone (SEK 190 plus SEK 250 per year). Flying without the required certificate has been punishable since 1 Jan 2026.
(b) Recommend the DJI Mini 5 Pro (1-inch, 50 MP, C0 with the standard battery only) over the Mini 4 Pro as the default C0 class.
(c) Resolve the viewer open question: Spark 2.x sorts multiple splat layers together with LoD (1.5M desktop / 500K mobile budget), so (c) can cross-fade layers without a hard switch. Keep the CHM-based volume partition to avoid double surfaces, and budget the shared mobile splat count.
(d) In the (b) technique, pair LightGlue only with ALIKED (BSD-3) or DISK (Apache), never SuperPoint (restrictive license). Add VGGT-1B-Commercial and MapAnything-apache as permissive learned-pose options, while noting they fail under large altitude gaps.
(e) Downgrade the plan to 'reimplement Horizon-GS sampling and densification in gsplat' for (b). Its measured benefit is aerial-only, so a loose merge with pose_opt is enough as the research experiment.
(f) Make the capture-timing advice more precise. Brown 2024 found overcast best only for sparse-forest CHM in summer, clear sky best for denser forest, and autumn clear sky best for DEMs. Overcast is still right for splat appearance consistency.
(g) Treat the claims on rotor downwash and Lowe's 50 deg figure as inference or background knowledge.
(h) Treat the UC-GS and CityGS-X code as unusable for a commercial path: UC-GS is confirmed Inria non-commercial, and CityGS-X has no license at all.

Caveat: the shared web-search budget ran out before this check started, so I could not search for new real-data aerial+ground forest splat studies. All verification above used direct fetches of primary pages.
