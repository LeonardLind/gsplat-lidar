# drone-forest-canopy

## Summary

BOTTOM LINE: A drone camera above a forest records the canopy top, crown shapes, gaps, terrain in open areas and the surroundings. It records very little of the forest floor or understory, and almost none in tropical rainforest. Aerial and under-canopy ground views share almost no surfaces, so putting them into one SfM/splat (option b) is the riskiest and least useful choice for this project. Recommendation: keep the first test as (a) LiDAR + Insta360. Add the drone later as an optional, separately reconstructed AERIAL LAYER (option c). Register it to the LiDAR frame and show it in the same viewer with fading based on camera height and region. Do not merge it into the ground splat.

VISIBILITY THROUGH CANOPY. Forestry remote-sensing work agrees: UAV photogrammetry (SfM/DAP) rebuilds only the visible top-of-canopy surface. It gets ground points only where gaps are big enough to be lit and seen from the oblique angles that triangulation needs. Wallace et al. 2016 (eucalypt): as canopy got denser, SfM gave <1 ground point/m² while UAV LiDAR still mapped the terrain. Jayathunga et al. 2018: where non-ground cover was >0.95, DAP-only estimates broke down (R²=0.20). With a LiDAR DTM they reached R²=0.91. In tropical rainforest, Swinfield et al. 2019 (Borneo) say it is 'often impossible' to map the ground from SfM because gaps are rare. Roşca et al. 2018 (Guyana) found that UAV SfM overstated heights inside gaps and missed small gaps altogether. Rainforest canopies block about 96% of laser shots and leave a few % of light at the floor (Amazon example: 673 ground returns out of more than 600k). Swedish boreal/hemiboreal stands are much more open, but they vary by species. Measured canopy transmittance is 0.12–0.89 in Scots pine and 0.04–0.40 in spruce (British data used as a proxy). The Swedish national programme estimates forest variables from aerial image matching plus a separate ALS DEM, because image matching gives the canopy surface but not the terrain. Expected result: in a thinned pine or birch stand, oblique drone images will show some trunks and ground patches. TreeDGS measured DBH from 70/90 m oblique Matrice 4E images in a pine plantation, but the breast-height band was often hidden and fully hidden stems could not be recovered. In spruce, and in any tropical forest, the drone gives canopy, terrain and context only. Drone LiDAR gets through much better (understory DEM RMSE ≤0.24 m where cover is <0.9), but it is a different sensor class.

WHAT DRONE IMAGERY IS GOOD FOR: top-of-canopy height and crown shapes, gaps, forest edges, terrain and context around the plot, and an overview mode in the viewer. It also fills a real hole in ground-only capture. Handheld and terrestrial scanning hides the upper crowns and underestimates tree height (one HMLS study: bias −2.34 m). Aerial and terrestrial clouds together cover a wider range of tree heights (Casseau/Fallon IROS 2024). This matters most in tropical forests, where a BLK2GO and an Insta360 at 1.5 m height will never see the top of a canopy 30–50 m up.

MERGED SPLAT PROBLEMS (evidence comes mostly from urban scenes, which are easier than forests). COLMAP-class SfM fails between aerial and ground images. DRAGON: plain COLMAP registered only about 50% of a drone+ground set, and it worked only after 'bridge' images at intermediate heights were added. AerialMegaDepth: DUSt3R placed fewer than 5% of aerial-ground pairs within 5°. SIFT often finds zero correct matches. Horizon-GS (CVPR 2025) reports that joint training causes conflicting densification: street views prune Gaussians that aerial views regrow. The jointly trained model scored below both separately trained ones. It also needed ContextCapture poses, extra transitional views and A100-80GB GPUs, and it uses the non-commercial Inria license. The commercial XGRIDS aerial-ground fusion requires an RTK fix on both devices plus 'vertical photo towers' (ascent sequences) as bridges. A BLK2GO has no GNSS, RTK under canopy is unreliable, and bridging ascents are only possible in clearings or at forest edges. In a forest, the drone sees the tops of the crowns and the ground camera sees the trunks and the undersides of the crowns, so there are almost no shared features. One 2026 paper did fuse handheld SLAM LiDAR with UAV imagery in a 3DGS forest twin (PSNR 24.94), but it aligned through point-cloud registration and LiDAR anchors, not through joint image matching. Because the viewer limits navigation to the LiDAR ground, aerial Gaussians in a merged model would mostly be seen from below, through the lower canopy. Expected gain at walking height: small. Risk of floaters, double canopy and lost detail: real.

SEPARATE LAYER (option c). Train the aerial splat, or build a mesh/DSM/orthophoto, with standard single-domain tools. Register it to the LiDAR frame using GCP targets placed in gaps and scanned by the BLK2GO (cm level), drone RTK/PPK georeferencing, ICP of the drone canopy and gap-ground points against the upper LiDAR points, or tree-position matching (0.31–0.44 m RMSE in published work). Expect 0.1–0.5 m residuals. Avoid content overlap: crop the aerial layer to the canopy top and surroundings, crop the ground splat above the canopy, and fade between them by camera height above the LiDAR DTM. Current web renderers handle two splat objects correctly. Spark sorts all SplatMeshes in one global sort, Spark 2.0 (Apr 2026) runs joint LoD across several objects under one budget (500K–2.5M splats), and SplatMesh has a global opacity. PlayCanvas unified rendering also sorts all GSplat components together. GaussianSplats3D is unmaintained. Separate training also keeps peak VRAM within 16 GB.

UNDER-CANOPY DRONE. Research systems work but are fragile. An autonomous Finnish prototype succeeded on 87.5% of missions in a difficult stand of 2,000 trees/ha, with emergency stops caused by thin dry branches, a trajectory error of 0.34–0.50 m and 1 m/s flight. Consumer options: DJI Avata 360 (Mar 2026, 455 g, 8K/60 360°, omnidirectional sensing, prop guards) and Antigravity A1 (249 g, 8K 360°, but only front and bottom sensing). For this project, handheld 360 capture alongside the BLK2GO is better: it follows the same path, is easier to time-sync and has no crash risk. To add camera heights of 2–5 m, a telescopic pole is a cheaper option (not researched; background knowledge). In tropical understory the low light, vines and need for VLOS (visual line of sight) make it worse still.

VERDICTS. Sweden: (a) for the first test. A drone gives little benefit for a 10×10 m plot. If context is wanted, try free Lantmäteriet open data first (ALS point cloud, 1–2 pts/m²; 1 m DTM; 0.16/0.4 m orthophotos) as a context layer, then (c) as an optional phase. Tropical: (c) is clearly worth it for canopy-top documentation, but never for the floor. (b) is not recommended in either case, except as research at a forest edge or clearing with bridging captures.

## Recommendations

- **Keep the first Swedish test as option (a), LiDAR + Insta360 only. Do not buy a drone or plan flights for phase 1.** — A 10x10 m plot gains little from above: the viewer limits navigation to the LiDAR ground, so aerial content would mostly be seen from below through the lower canopy. Merging is the riskiest path: joint SfM fails, densification conflicts, and the BLK2GO has no RTK. Phase 1 should prove registering the splat to the LiDAR first. _Alternatives:_ If the user wants context in phase 1, add a zero-cost 'aerial context' layer from Lantmäteriet open ALS, DTM and orthophoto instead of flying.
- **Make the drone a later, optional phase built as option (c): a separate aerial model (splat for photoreal views, plus optional DSM/orthophoto or mesh) registered into the LiDAR frame and shown as its own viewer layer. Do not choose (b), one merged splat.** — Published evidence points to separate reconstruction followed by combination. Horizon-GS shows joint training below both single-domain models, CrossView-GS uses separate branches, and DRAGON/AerialMegaDepth show joint registration failing. Forests have even less shared content between aerial and ground views. Separate training also fits 16 GB VRAM, uses standard open tools for each source, and matches the modular source architecture. _Alternatives:_ (b) only as a research experiment at a forest edge or clearing, with bridging 'photo tower' ascents and targets, and a LiDAR-anchored joint optimisation like the 2026 forest digital-twin paper. Expect a lot of engineering and possible quality loss.
- **Plug the drone module into the common source interface with these outputs: a reconstruction (splat .ply/.spz/.rad, optional DSM/ortho GeoTIFF or mesh), a 4x4 similarity transform into the shared frame with residuals, a coverage polygon, a valid height band (e.g. canopy top and above), and its georeference if any. Rules for the shared frame: LiDAR frame when LiDAR is present; otherwise the drone's georeferenced frame (GNSS/RTK EXIF gives metric scale and orientation); otherwise an arbitrary frame.** — The drone is the only source that can carry an absolute georeference (BLK2GO has no GNSS). Treating the georeference as optional metadata on the shared frame lets the 'drone only' and 'drone + splat' combinations work without LiDAR. _Alternatives:_ Always force a georeferenced ENU frame and attach the LiDAR to it. This adds a GCP or RTK dependency for every combination that includes LiDAR.
- **Register drone to LiDAR in order of preference: (1) 3–5 large targets (checkerboard, ≥0.5 m) placed in canopy gaps or at the plot edge, seen by the drone and scanned or photographed by the BLK2GO and Insta360 (cm level); (2) drone RTK/PPK georeferencing plus ICP of the drone dense cloud (gap ground and canopy) against the upper LiDAR points; (3) markerless matching of tree positions or canopy shape as a fallback (expect 0.3–0.5 m). Keep the user-measured device offset and a software check, as in the existing ground alignment.** — Natural-feature aerial-to-terrestrial registration in forests reaches only 0.31–0.44 m RMSE, which shows visibly if the layers overlap. Targets give cm-level results. Joint image matching is not viable under canopy. _Alternatives:_ Joint SfM with ground images, possible only where the plot touches a clearing and bridging ascent images are captured.
- **Viewer composition: split the content so the two layers do not overlap. Crop the aerial layer to the canopy top and the area outside the plot, and crop ground-splat floaters above the canopy. Fade the aerial layer in by camera height above the LiDAR DTM (e.g. hidden below canopy base, full above canopy top) using SplatMesh.opacity. Render both under one Spark 2.0 budget with joint LoD, or use PlayCanvas unified mode.** — Spark and PlayCanvas sort all splat objects together, so sorting is solved. The remaining artifacts are double canopy and ghosting from decimetre misregistration, and these are avoided by not overlapping the content. Shared budgets keep phones at about 0.5–1M splats. _Alternatives:_ Show the aerial model as a textured mesh or DSM plus orthophoto (lighter, robust on mobile, good for LuciadRIA) and keep splats only for the ground.
- **Do not use an under-canopy drone for understory capture. If extra camera heights are wanted for mid-canopy, use the Insta360 on a 3–5 m telescopic pole along the BLK2GO loop. Consider a 360 drone with omnidirectional sensing and guards (e.g. DJI Avata 360) only for forest-edge or clearing 'bridge' shots.** — Under-canopy drones work in research but are fragile: thin branches go undetected and there is no GNSS. Handheld capture keeps the same path and time sync as the LiDAR. Antigravity A1 lacks side, rear and up sensing. _Alternatives:_ Manually piloted cinewhoop (Avata 2) at walking pace for 2–6 m heights in open pine stands only, with a spotter.
- **Tropical phase: plan the drone as a canopy-top and context layer only (option c). Fly from a clearing, river or road where VLOS and takeoff are possible. Never expect it to add understory or floor content. If terrain under canopy is needed beyond the BLK2GO's reach, budget for drone LiDAR rather than photos.** — Rainforest canopy blocks about 96% of laser shots and SfM rarely reaches the ground. The ground devices cannot see a canopy top 30–50 m up, so in the tropics the drone adds the most information. _Alternatives:_ Skip the aerial layer and accept a ground-only model with a blurry or absent canopy top.
- **Capture protocol hints for a later drone phase (to be reconciled with the capture-strategy and regulations research): grid plus oblique passes (camera about 45–60° off nadir) and one or two orbits around the plot at 30–70 m AGL. Use ≥90% forward and ≥80–85% side overlap, still photos rather than video, and a fixed exposure set for the canopy. Fly within about 1 h of the ground capture, preferably under uniform overcast, with low wind. In Sweden, a leaf-off flight sees more floor in birch and aspen but will not match a leaf-on ground splat.** — Forest SfM completeness rises with overlap up to about 95%. Obliques improve gap and stem visibility (TreeDGS, Swinfield). Matching light reduces the appearance gap between layers. _Alternatives:_ Single nadir grid only, which gives a DSM and orthophoto but a poor splat from oblique viewpoints.

## Key numbers

- Canopy transmittance (proxy for vertical floor visibility): Scots pine 0.12–0.89; Sitka spruce 0.04–0.40 (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237267640_Relationships_between_canopy_transmittance_and_stand_parameters_in_Sitka_spruce_and_Scots_pine_stands_in_Britain)
- Amazon rainforest canopy cover / gap fraction / PAR at 1 m: about 96% / about 4% / about 5.7% (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168192319302692)
- Laser shots blocked: thick tropical vs temperate forest: up to 96% vs 70–80% (https://www.gim-international.com/content/article/airborne-lidar-in-rainforest)
- UAV SfM vs TLS top-of-canopy agreement (Guyana): within 0.5 m in 71–77% of pixels; gap overestimate up to +11.42 m (99th pct) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5829181/)
- UAV LiDAR understory DEM error for canopy cover <0.9: MAE ≤0.17 m, RMSE ≤0.24 m (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11629-024-9436-8)

## risks

- Merged splat (b): joint SfM between canopy-top and under-canopy images is likely to fail or mis-register; densification conflicts degrade both views; floaters and a double canopy appear where the layers overlap.
- Separate layer (c): decimetre misregistration (0.3–0.5 m with natural features) shows as ghosting unless the layer content is kept apart by cropping, or targets visible from both views are used.
- Under-canopy RTK/GNSS is unreliable, and the BLK2GO has no GNSS, so commercial-style RTK fusion cannot be used.
- Appearance mismatch: sunlit canopy top vs shaded understory (transmittance 2–90%), and different capture times, seasons or wind states between drone and ground capture.
- Wind moves crowns between drone images, which adds SfM and splat noise in the canopy; overlap below about 80–90% gives holes.
- Under-canopy drone flight: collisions with thin branches, GNSS-denied drift, prop wash moving foliage (background knowledge), low light causing blur; possible VLOS problems in dense or tropical forest.
- Tropical: no takeoff space or VLOS under a 30–50 m canopy; pilot needs a clearing or edge; aerial layer covers canopy only.
- Research methods for unified aerial-ground GS (Horizon-GS) carry non-commercial licenses and need 80 GB GPUs, so they are not usable on an RTX 3080 Ti 16 GB or in a Hexagon context.
- Mobile render budget (about 0.5–1M splats) must be shared between layers, and the aerial layer can crowd out ground detail unless LoD and budget priorities are tuned.
- Legal/dissemination: publishing aerial imagery from Swedish drone flights on the web may need a dissemination permit (spridningstillstånd) under the Swedish Act on protection of geographic information (2016:319); this could not be checked (search budget exhausted) and must be confirmed by the regulations research.

## open_questions

- Web search budget ran out during this research; Swedish dissemination-permit rules (spridningstillstånd) for drone aerial imagery, EU class label of the DJI Avata 360, and the exact CC0 license status of Lantmäteriet orthophoto and laser products are unverified.
- For the 2026 handheld-LiDAR + UAV-imagery forest 3DGS paper (Remote Sens. 18(11):1696), the UAV altitude and whether it flew above or below canopy are unknown (full text blocked); per-view quality was not compared with separate layers.
- There is no published quantitative study of a merged aerial + under-canopy ground splat in a forest versus separate layers; the forest-specific merge conclusions are inferred from urban evidence plus canopy-occlusion physics.
- Actual canopy cover and species mix of the user's Swedish test stand (pine, spruce or birch dominated?) decide how much floor and how many trunks a drone would see.
- Whether the BLK2GO's range (around 25 m) captures enough upper-crown points to ICP-register a drone canopy cloud in the Swedish stand, and certainly not in tropical canopies of 30–50 m.
- Whether the viewer will offer an 'overview/fly-above' mode at all; if navigation stays at ground level, the aerial layer's visible value drops further.

## questions_for_user

- Will the viewer allow users to rise above the canopy (overview or fly mode), or is navigation always at walking height within LiDAR ground boundaries? This decides whether an aerial layer is worth building at all.
- What dominates the Swedish test stand: Scots pine, Norway spruce or birch? Is it thinned or near a clearing or forest edge where bridging drone shots could be taken?
- Is a free aerial context layer from Lantmäteriet open data (ALS point cloud, 1 m DTM, 0.16/0.4 m orthophoto) acceptable as a stand-in for the drone in the Swedish phase?
- For the tropical phase, is documenting the canopy top (crowns, gaps, emergent trees) a goal, or only the understory experience? Do you have access to clearings or rivers for takeoff and VLOS?
- Is georeferencing (placing the scene on a map, e.g. in LuciadRIA) a requirement? If yes, a drone with RTK/PPK, or ground targets surveyed by GNSS in a clearing, becomes the georeference source.

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The core recommendations hold: (a) LiDAR + Insta360 for the first Swedish test, the drone as a later optional SEPARATE aerial layer (c), and no merged splat (b). The independently verified evidence agrees. Horizon-GS states that joint training is worse than separate training and needs transitional views, ContextCapture poses, an A100 80 GB and a non-commercial license. DRAGON (about 50% COLMAP registration without bridge images), AerialMegaDepth (<5% of pairs within 5°) and the 3DGS intermediate-view paper (0 correct SIFT/SuperGlue matches) confirm that aerial-ground matching fails. XGRIDS fusion needs an RTK fix on both devices and an XGRIDS scanner, so it cannot be used. The only merged forest result (RS 2026) was LiDAR-anchored and tested only in open canopy. The viewer claims (Spark 2.0 global sort with joint multi-object LoD and SplatMesh.opacity; PlayCanvas global sort) are confirmed.

Changes I would make:
1. Make the Swedish dissemination permit an explicit gate for the drone phase. It is not 'maybe'. Web publication of drone imagery or derived splats needs a Lantmäteriet permit (about 65 working days, free). Only the submitted and returned material may be used for derivative products. The forest exemption does not cover zoomed-out context views, so plan the submission into the schedule. This also strengthens the 'use Lantmäteriet open data as the context layer first' advice, since Lantmäteriet already publishes that data. Verify the per-product license (CC0 vs CC BY 4.0).
2. Correct the capture protocol altitude. '30–70 m AGL' is too low and is stated relative to the wrong reference. Frey 2018 (completeness better with coarser GSD and >95% overlap), Forests 2024 (120 m better than 80 m, 90/85 overlap) and TreeDGS (70/90 m) point to about 70–110 m AGL with ≥90/85 overlap plus obliques. State it as height above canopy top (aim for ≥45–60 m clearance). It is capped by the EU Open-category 120 m limit measured from the ground (background knowledge, high confidence). Over a 30–50 m tropical canopy, 30–70 m AGL would put the drone at or just above emergent crowns, which is unsafe and gives poor SfM.
3. Temper the registration accuracy ladder. The 0.31–0.44 m markerless figures are LiDAR-to-LiDAR (ULS vs backpack). A photogrammetric canopy registered to BLK2GO points will likely be worse, given SfM gap and height bias (Roşca, Swinfield). The '0.4 cm target registration in Cyclone' figure is unverified. Plan for GCP targets in gaps or edges as the primary method, and confirm that the BLK2GO can localise target centres to cm level.
4. Add the TreeDGS nuance. In thinned pine, oblique aerial GS recovered stems better than drone LiDAR, so a pine-site drone pilot may yield more trunk and ground content than 'canopy only'. This raises the value of (c) but does not justify (b).
5. Fix factual slips: Swinfield's site is Sumatra (Indonesia), not Sabah. Antigravity A1 is C1 with the high-capacity battery and about 2.7K/1080p per direction. Avata 360 is EU C1 (confirmed). The Horizon-GS repo has Windows install steps. The GNSS-under-canopy source is a master's thesis. The J. Mountain Sci. paper is from 2025.
6. Viewer: treat PlayCanvas unified mode as something to enable explicitly. Remember Spark's sort lags at least one frame during fast fades.
7. VRAM: '16 GB is enough for a separate aerial splat' depends on image count and resolution. TreeDGS used 32 GB for 365 images, so plan to downscale or tile the aerial training on the 3080 Ti.
8. Optionally mention DJI Terra 5.x built-in 3DGS as a turnkey aerial-layer path if a DJI Enterprise drone is chosen, after checking its export and license terms.

The tropical verdict (aerial layer only for canopy and context; drone LiDAR if terrain under canopy is needed) holds.
