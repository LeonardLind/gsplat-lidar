# time-sync

## Summary

Bottom line: neither device has a hardware sync path. The BLK2GO spec lists only a wireless app link and USB 3.0/USB-C, with no trigger, PPS or timecode port. The Insta360 X4/X5 can only take timecode in from an external generator through its mic adapter, which syncs the camera to that generator but not to the BLK2GO. A clap is heard only by the Insta360. Wall-clock metadata (MP4 creation time at 1 s resolution, the BLK2GO acquisition date/time, Insta360 timecode in HH:MM:SS:FF) is good to about ±1 s at best, so it can only narrow the search window. The only practical high-precision method is the frame-invariant one: cross-correlate the angular-velocity magnitude |omega| from the Insta360 gyro against |omega| derived from the BLK2GO SLAM trajectory orientation, then estimate the mount rotation and refine.

Insta360 side (good). The .insv trailer stores IMU samples at 1 kHz on the X5. They are on a monotonic microsecond capture clock that is shared with the per-frame exposure records (capture time of the middle row plus shutter) and with first_frame_timestamp. Raw-layout cameras (X5) use microseconds; older float-layout cameras use milliseconds. The info record holds a gyro-offset field (1.6 ms on the X5) and a rolling-shutter time; X5 readout is about 10.6 ms at 8K30. GyroView (MIT) documents the format and measured residual gyro-to-frame offsets of -0.4±1 ms (X6), +0.4±2.6 and +2.7±3.7 ms (X5), with one X5 outlier at -11±4 ms. Gyroflow states that Insta360 files are synced internally (v1.5.1 notes), but it does not support 360 cameras for stabilization.

Two critical pitfalls on the Insta360 side:
- **Capture clock vs. video timeline drift.** GyroView says the X5 capture clock drifts from the video track timeline by more than one frame in four minutes (about 140 ppm or more). Map frames to gyro time with the per-frame exposure records, never with frame index / fps.
- **"30 fps" is really 29.97.** X5 '30 fps' footage appears to be 30000/1001 (a GyroView keyframe at 98.098 s = 2940 x 1.001/30). Assuming exactly 30 fps gives about 1000 ppm error, roughly 0.9 s over 15 min.

Extract frames with FFmpeg using -fps_mode passthrough and -frame_pts 1, and keep the PTS of each extracted frame. Parsers: telemetry-parser (MIT/Apache-2.0) lists the X5 but not the X3/X4 in its README, and its PyPI wheel (0.3.0, Jan 2024) predates X5 support, so use a Rust build from master. Alternatives are ExifTool 13.59 (May 2026, -ee extracts Accelerometer/AngularVelocity/TimeCode from the Insta360 trailer), insvtool (Apache-2.0, Python), or a small custom Python parser following GyroView FORMAT.md. Also export any equirectangular video with FlowState/direction lock OFF (background knowledge). Otherwise image orientation is decoupled from body orientation, which breaks SfM-rotation checks and lever-arm estimation.

BLK2GO side (uncertain, and this is load-bearing). Track Editor has a time slider, waypoints show time elapsed from start, and track properties include duration and acquisition date/time. Trajectory CSV export exists (Cyclone REGISTER 360 PLUS 2023.1.0 / Cyclone FIELD 360), and there is a CLI -b2g2trajectory. E57 export includes the trajectory as a separate point cloud plus pano image poses (pano captured every 1 s, global shutter). Cyclone 3DR 2024.1+ handles per-point timestamps in LAS/LAZ/E57. However, I could not confirm from any primary source that the BLK2GO trajectory CSV contains per-pose timestamps and orientation, what its sample rate is, or whether LAS gps_time or E57 timeStamp are filled for BLK2GO points. Raw BLK2GO IMU export is not documented. A data audit on the first export therefore gates the method choice. If the trajectory has timestamps and quaternions at 10 Hz or more, the |omega| correlation works. If it only has timed positions, use position-based spatio-temporal alignment. If it has no time at all, fall back to time-free geometric registration, which the pipeline should use for final registration anyway.

Clock drift. Smartphone-class crystals drift less than 1.2 ms/min (about 20 ppm) across 47 models (RecSync, IEEE Sensors 2021). Budget 10–50 ppm between the BLK2GO and the Insta360, which is 9–45 ms over 15 min, so estimate both offset and rate. Use sync gestures at start and end plus sliding windows on corner turns, with a robust linear fit. Twist-n-Sync (Sensors 2021) shows gyro-correlation sync can reach microseconds between raw gyros. Here the limit is the BLK2GO trajectory rate and smoothing; the estimate (no published BLK2GO figure) is about 1–5 ms with 50 Hz or faster orientation and strong twists, and about 5–15 ms at 10 Hz.

Error budget. Position error is v·dt (1 mm per ms at 1 m/s). Rotation error is omega·dt (0.09° per ms at 90°/s, so 10 ms is 0.9°). On an 8K equirect (21.3 px/°), 0.9° is about 19 px; at 5 m range it is about 8 cm lateral. Lever-arm coupling is small (0.2 m x 0.9° is about 3 mm). A constant dt on a 10x10 m loop at 1 m/s biases a trajectory Sim3 yaw by about v·dt/R, roughly 0.11° per 10 ms, and biases a trajectory-estimated lever arm by v·dt along the track (1 cm per 10 ms). BLK2GO spec accuracy is ±10 mm indoor and ±3 mm range noise. Target 5 ms or better (acceptable up to 10 ms). Use LiDAR poses as soft priors with motion-inflated sigmas: sigma_pos² = s0² + (|v|·sigma_t)² and sigma_rot² = r0² + (|omega|·sigma_t)².

Recommended procedure:
- **At start:** start the Insta360 first, then start the BLK2GO and let SLAM initialize. Clap twice, then give one firm tap on the rig (audio plus accelerometer spike, which checks the camera's internal audio-to-IMU alignment). Hold still 5 s, do 3 sharp yaw twists (±20–30°, peak about 100–200°/s), then 3 pitch/roll twists, then hold still 5 s.
- **During the run:** walk the loop, making deliberate 90° corner turns.
- **At the end:** repeat the start sequence, stop the BLK2GO, then stop the Insta360.
- **Processing:**
  - Filter both |omega| signals with the identical zero-phase filter (butter, sosfiltfilt) and interpolate to a common grid of 100–200 Hz.
  - Coarse lag: normalized correlation via scipy.signal.correlate(method='fft') with correlation_lags, restricted to the gesture windows found by an RMS envelope.
  - Sub-sample refinement: parabolic peak interpolation, then minimize_scalar of 1-NCC on continuous interpolated signals.
  - Drift: per-window offsets fitted with scipy.stats.theilslopes, giving offset a and rate b in ppm.
  - Mount rotation: R_rel = Rotation.align_vectors(omega_blk, omega_ins(t+dt)), compared with the user's measured orientation; optionally refine dt jointly using vector residuals.
  - Verification: an independent dt from SfM-frame relative rotations against BLK2GO rotations, and start/end consistency within 2–3 ms after the drift model.
- **Not to rely on:** clap, wall clock, visual phone-clock events (unless BLK2GO image timestamps prove precise) and hardware sync.

## Recommendations

- **Day-0 data audit before building the sync module: from Cyclone REGISTER 360 PLUS 2026.1.1 (and/or Cyclone FIELD 360) export (a) trajectory CSV, (b) E57 via -b2g2trajectory with images, (c) LAS/LAZ. Check whether the trajectory has per-pose timestamps and quaternions and at what rate. Check E57 trajectory/point fields (timeStamp) and image acquisitionDateTime precision, and LAS point format and gps_time (laspy 2.7.0: las.point_format.dimension_names).** — The whole high-precision method depends on timestamped BLK2GO orientations, and public Leica docs do not specify the CSV columns or rate. _Alternatives:_ If only timed positions: position-based spatio-temporal alignment. If no time at all: time-free geometric registration only.
- **PRIMARY method: cross-correlate |omega| from the Insta360 1 kHz gyro with |omega| from the BLK2GO trajectory, then refine.
1. Identical zero-phase low-pass on both (scipy.signal.butter(4, fc≈5-8 Hz and ≤0.4x BLK2GO rate, output='sos') + sosfiltfilt), then interpolate to a common 100-200 Hz grid.
2. Coarse lag: z-score, then scipy.signal.correlate(method='fft') + correlation_lags, restricted to gesture windows found by a moving-RMS envelope.
3. Sub-sample: parabolic interpolation, delta = 0.5(c[k-1]-c[k+1])/(c[k-1]-2c[k]+c[k+1]).
4. Fine: scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar(bounded, ±50 ms) of 1-NCC on continuously interpolated signals.
5. Drift: offsets per window (start gesture, end gesture, each corner turn) fitted with scipy.stats.theilslopes to dt(t)=a+b·t; report b in ppm.
6. Mount rotation: R_rel via Rotation.align_vectors; optionally re-estimate dt jointly from vector residuals ‖omega_b(t+dt) - R_rel·omega_i(t)‖².** — Frame-invariant, needs no target, uses data both devices actually record, and is a proven method (hand_eye_calibration, Twist-n-Sync, Gyroflow). Expected 1-5 ms with good excitation. _Alternatives:_ Reuse ethz-asl/hand_eye_calibration time_alignment.py (BSD-3) as a reference implementation; Kalibr is not applicable (needs a target and raw IMU from both).
- **Capture protocol, identical at start and end of every run:
1. Start the Insta360 first, recording .insv.
2. Start the BLK2GO and let SLAM initialize while standing still in a feature-rich spot.
3. Clap twice near the Insta360, then give one firm tap on the rig.
4. Hold still 5 s.
5. Do 3 sharp yaw twists (±20-30°, about 0.5 s each, peak about 100-200°/s), pause 2 s, then 3 pitch and 3 roll twists.
6. Hold still 5 s.
7. Walk the loop with deliberate 90° corner turns; keep runs ≤15 min.
8. At the end, repeat steps 3-6, stop the BLK2GO, then stop the Insta360.
Use a rigid mount, not tape, when estimating R_rel.** — Still periods give gyro bias and clean baselines. Twists give broadband rotation on all axes (needed for R_rel). Start and end events give both offset and drift. Corner turns give mid-run checks. The clap/tap marks the window and checks audio-to-IMU alignment. _Alternatives:_ If twists degrade BLK2GO SLAM in testing, reduce to ±15° at ~0.7 Hz and rely more on corner turns.
- **Frame timing hygiene: map every extracted video frame to the Insta360 capture clock via exposure records (capture time + exposure/2, plus the gyro offset field). Never use frame_index/30. Extract with ffmpeg -fps_mode passthrough and image2 -frame_pts 1 (or log PTS with showinfo). Export equirect untrimmed with FlowState and direction lock OFF, and verify the export against the .insv (frame count, audio cross-correlation).** — The X5 capture clock drifts versus the track timeline by more than one frame in about 4 min, and 30 fps is likely 29.97. Either mistake gives tens to hundreds of ms of error, more than any sync method's error. _Alternatives:_ Stitch from raw .insv yourself (GyroView approach) to keep full control of frame times.
- **VERIFICATION:
(a) start-vs-end offsets agree within 2-3 ms after the drift model, and |b| < 100 ppm;
(b) SfM-based check: relative rotations between SfM-registered frames (5-10 fps, frame times from exposure records), mapped through R_rel, give an independent dt and a rotation residual below about 0.3-0.5°;
(c) R_rel within about 1-2° of the user's measured mount orientation;
(d) report peak NCC (>0.9 on gestures) and bootstrap sigma_dt over windows.** — These are independent checks that catch wrong frame-time mapping, non-rigid mounting and smoothed or lagged SLAM orientation, and they produce the 'do the estimates agree' report the user wants. _Alternatives:_ Phone ms-clock visual event seen by BLK2GO pano and Insta360, only if E57 image timestamps prove precise.
- **BACKUP method: if the BLK2GO trajectory has timed positions but no usable orientation, register SfM to LiDAR geometrically (time-free), then estimate dt (and lever arm) by minimizing ‖p_sfm(t) - p_blk(t+dt) - R_blk(t+dt)·l‖ over all frames (scipy.optimize.least_squares).** — At 1 m/s, 1 cm residual corresponds to about 10 ms. This is coarser than gyro correlation but works with positions only and reuses the registration the pipeline needs anyway. _Alternatives:_ Gesture-envelope alignment, about 20-50 ms precision.
- **Use LiDAR poses as soft priors in SfM/BA with motion-inflated sigmas: sigma_pos² = s0² + (|v|·sigma_t)², sigma_rot² = r0² + (|omega|·sigma_t)², with sigma_t taken from the sync uncertainty. Down-weight or skip rotation priors during fast turns.** — At 90°/s even 5 ms gives 0.45° (about 10 px at 8K equirect), while position error is only 5 mm. Rotation priors are the sync-sensitive part. _Alternatives:_ Position-only priors.
- **Parse Insta360 IMU with a pinned toolchain tested on the actual camera model: telemetry-parser built from master (Rust; PyPI 0.3.0 is from Jan 2024 and predates X5), or ExifTool 13.59 -ee, or a small custom Python trailer parser following GyroView FORMAT.md.** — Model support differs: the telemetry-parser README lists X5 but not X3/X4, while GyroView supports X3/X4 Air/X5/X6. _Alternatives:_ insvtool (Apache-2.0, Python) for JSON dumps.

## risks

- BLK2GO export may lack timestamped orientations, or be decimated or smoothed, making |omega| correlation infeasible or imprecise. Mitigation: Day-0 audit plus position-based or time-free fallback.
- Wrong Insta360 frame timing (PTS x 30 instead of exposure records; 29.97 vs 30; capture-clock drift) causes errors of tens to hundreds of ms, larger than any sync method error.
- FlowState/direction lock in exported equirect decouples image orientation from body orientation, which invalidates SfM-rotation checks and lever-arm estimation.
- Mismatched or causal filtering of the two |omega| signals biases the offset. Always apply an identical zero-phase filter (sosfiltfilt) to both.
- Aggressive sync twists may degrade BLK2GO SLAM (LiDAR motion distortion, visual SLAM blur).
- A taped (non-rigid) mount flexes during twists, which lowers correlation, biases R_rel, and makes the lever arm time-varying.
- Clock drift changes with temperature (cold Swedish autumn vs tropics). A start-only offset will be wrong by about 10-45 ms by the end of a 15-min run.
- Rolling shutter (about 10.6 ms readout on X5 8K30) blurs the definition of 'frame time' at the 5 ms level and skews frames by about 1° at 90°/s.
- Third-party parsers (telemetry-parser, GyroView, ExifTool) can break on new firmware or camera models. Pin versions and validate on a sample clip.
- Web search budget was exhausted mid-research. The Twist-n-Sync and Kalibr accuracy figures and the BLK2GO CSV columns could not be verified further from primary sources.

## FACT-CHECK: non-confirmed verdicts

- **CORRECTED**: Residual gyro-to-frame offsets: X6 -0.4±1.0 ms, X5 +0.4±2.6 and +2.7±3.7 ms, with one X5 outlier at -11±4 ms. X5 rolling-shutter readout is ~10.6 ms at 8K30, and 4–10.6 ms depending on mode. → ADR 0034 says the office (+2.7±3.7) and krnjaca (-11±4) recordings were 'too unstable for reliable measurement' (SE >3 ms). ## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The core recommendations hold up. There is no hardware sync on either device. The Day-0 BLK2GO export audit gates everything. The primary method is |omega| cross-correlation followed by drift (offset + rate) estimation from start and end windows, with time-free geometric registration as the final Sim3. Frame timing must come from Insta360 exposure records. The error-budget arithmetic is correct.

What I would change:

1. **BLK2GO data path.** Expect that a high-rate timestamped orientation stream may not exist. E57 pano poses are spaced by distance (WayPoint interval in metres), not time, so they cannot drive |omega| correlation; drop them as a fallback for that purpose. Audit four routes in this order:
   - (a) FIELD 360 / REGISTER 360 trajectory CSV header;
   - (b) LAS point format and gps_time via laspy;
   - (c) E57 trajectory-cloud fields (timeStamp) via pye57;
   - (d) Cyclone 3DR import of LGSx/LAS to see whether trajectories or points carry time.
   Also note that REGISTER 360 PLUS 2026.1.1 dropped the BLK2GO driver, so the import path is now USB.

2. **Gesture design.** Three identical twists about 0.5 s apart produce strong correlation side-lobes at ±0.5–1 s. The wall-clock window (±1 s) cannot resolve that ambiguity. Use an aperiodic pattern: irregular intervals (for example 0.4 s / 1.1 s / 0.7 s) and different amplitudes per twist. Get the coarse lag from the whole run (aperiodic corner turns) before refining on the gesture windows. Do the twists after SLAM has initialized and after a few metres of walking, at moderate rates (about 100°/s). The claim that GrandSLAM tolerates sharp twists is untested, and Leica's general 'move steadily' guidance is background knowledge only.

3. **Frame-timing numbers.** The X5 capture-clock-versus-track drift is a single third-party observation, ≥~63–140 ppm depending on frame rate. The -11 ms 'outlier' was a failed measurement. The 29.97 claim should be verified with ffprobe, not assumed. Log real PTS with ffprobe -show_frames rather than trusting frame_pts file names. Report the measured rate b instead of hard-failing at a fixed |b| < 100 ppm, but confirm that the Insta360 capture clock, not the track clock, is the one being compared.

4. **Pose priors.** COLMAP 4.x supports position priors with covariance and gravity priors only. Rotation priors need a custom BA, so make position-only priors the default. Take gravity priors from the Insta360's own accelerometer; that needs no cross-device sync.

5. **Tool facts.**
   - telemetry-parser master handles the X4 as well as the X5; build it with maturin and pin the commit.
   - Gyroflow v1.6.3 is from 2025-09-04, not 2024.
   - SciPy is at 1.18.1 and needs Python ≥3.12.
   - Use ETH time_alignment.py only as an algorithm reference: it is ROS/Python-2-era code with an integer-sample peak.
   - Fix the inconsistent dt sign convention and check the BLK quaternion convention before Rotation.align_vectors.

6. **Camera choice.** If the model is still open, the X5 currently has the best independently verified gyro and timing tooling.

The web-search budget was exhausted early. All checks above were done by fetching primary sources directly. The BLK2GO CSV columns and timestamps, and the Twist-n-Sync content, remain unverified.
