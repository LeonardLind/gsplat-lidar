# forest-risks

## Summary

State of the art (Oct 2026) for Gaussian splatting (GS) plus LiDAR in forests, and what it means for a 10x10 m Swedish loop test that will later scale to tropical plots.

1) Forest GS quality. Recent forest GS work (TreeDGS 2026, LiDAR-Guided Semantic 3DGS for Forest Digital Twins May 2026, ForestSplat mangroves, Sapling-NeRF Feb 2026) keeps finding the same failures: floaters (Gaussians with nonzero opacity in free space), sheet-like foliage artifacts, thin stems a few pixels wide that never get reconstructed, and invented ("hallucinated") geometry in occluded regions. Generic SIFT matching is weak because trunks have little texture and foliage keeps occluding them. TreeDGS needed a forest-trained matcher plus GLOMAP. Adding LiDAR helps: the forest digital-twin paper used LiDAR anchors for initialization plus trunk/canopy semantic regularization (PSNR 24.94, DBH RMSE 2.7 cm). A review reports 20-30% accuracy gains from LiDAR fusion. Expect good results on ground, trunks and mid-canopy, fuzzy fine foliage, and a weak upper canopy and sky. Neither modality covers the canopy top well: mobile LiDAR tree-height RMSE is 1.83 m in one study.

2) Wind and transients. SpotLessSplats (Apache-2.0, a fork of gsplat) explicitly targets wind-blown elements. It gives +1 to +6.5 dB on distractor benchmarks but needs precomputed Stable Diffusion features and misses thin structures. Splatfacto-W (Apache-2.0, nerfstudio) has a residual-percentile robust mask plus an SH background/sky model, but nerfstudio's last release was Nov 2024. DeSplat is MIT on nerfstudio. WildGaussians, RobustSplat and T-3DGS are built on the Inria rasterizer, which is non-commercial only, so the user should evaluate them, not ship them. RobustSplat's main insight (delay densification so Gaussians do not grow to fit transients) can be reproduced in gsplat by changing refinement start steps. The only real fix for leaf motion is capture discipline: a calm day, a short capture and slow walking. True 4D wind modelling is still research (Sep 2026 papers).

3) Lighting. Lock exposure and white balance. Overcast light is strongly preferred. PPISP (NVIDIA, Apache-2.0, Jan 2026) has day-0 gsplat support and is in 3DGRUT. It models per-frame exposure and white balance plus per-camera vignetting and CRF, and has a controller for novel views. It beats bilateral grids on novel views (bilateral grids overfit per-frame). Key design point: virtual cameras cut from the same 360 frame must share one per-frame ISP parameter set (per lens). Reprojected virtual views break the radial-vignetting assumption, so either use the raw dual-fisheye or disable or freeze vignetting.

4) SfM and SLAM. COLMAP 4.1.0 (2026-06-26) added native spherical camera models but says they are less accurate than the panorama_sfm rig-of-perspective-views route. 4.2.0/4.2.1 (Sep 2026) add learned LoMa features through ONNX. Using 360 helps under canopy: it always sees ground and trunks in several directions, and loop closure is easy. It also hurts: the operator and the BLK2GO are always in frame, the sky fills the upper views, the stitch seam distorts geometry, and an 8K equirect gives only about 21 px/deg (a 90-degree view is about 1920 px wide). BLK2GO SLAM drift is a minor risk for a 10x10 m loop: tropical 1-ha plots showed 3.2-4.7 cm loop-closure residuals, and outdoor accuracy is 10-60 mm vs 5 mm indoors. For walks over 100 m it is a real design factor. LiDAR noise in foliage (mixed pixels at silhouettes, wind-smeared leaves) mainly corrupts ICP and depth supervision. Restrict registration and depth loss to ground and stems.

5) VRAM. gsplat (Apache-2.0; PyPI 1.5.3 Jul 2025; main bumped to 1.6.0 Aug 2026) uses about 4x less memory than Inria. On Mip-NeRF360 (A100): default strategy 3.24M Gaussians in 5.62 GB; MCMC 1M / 2M / 3M Gaussians in 1.98 / 3.43 / 4.99 GB. That is roughly 1.5-1.7 GB per million Gaussians at that resolution. Images stream from CPU via DataLoader, so image count barely affects VRAM; resolution and Gaussian count do. On the 16 GB laptop 3080 Ti, use MCMC with cap_max 2-4M, about 1.5-2 MP views, plus packed/absgrad. Expect the 10x10 m test to fit. Tropical plots will not fit in one model. The best chunked or offload trainers are all non-commercial: CityGS (CC BY-NC-SA), Hierarchical 3DGS (Inria), CLM (Inria; 100M+ Gaussians on a 4090 with 128 GB RAM), A LoD of Gaussians (CC BY-NC-ND). So plan to build your own spatial tiling on gsplat. Spark 2.3.1 (MIT, 2026-10-01) provides LoD and paged streaming (.RAD chunks). Its default LoD budgets are 1M splats on Android, 1.5M on iOS and 2.5M on desktop, with about 30M input splats per LoD build.

6) Sync and lever arm. A clap cannot sync the BLK2GO because it has no audio. Use a motion signature: a deliberate rotation wiggle while otherwise still. Cross-correlate angular-velocity magnitude from SfM camera rotations against the BLK2GO trajectory; this is scale-free. At 0.5-1 m/s and 30-90 deg/s turns, one 33 ms frame of error is 1.7-3.3 cm and 1-3 degrees. Translation lever arm is only observable with rotation about at least two non-parallel axes, so add pitch and roll wiggles. Tape mounts flex, so estimate the extrinsic per time window to detect this.

Design-in now for the tropics: LiDAR-trajectory pose priors with SfM only for refinement, LiDAR initialization plus confidence-masked depth loss, permissive-license tiled training, per-frame ISP grouping, LoD export, and dew/fog-proof capture procedures.

## Recommendations

- **RISK R1 - Blurry foliage, floaters, missing thin branches. Likelihood (10x10 m Sweden): HIGH. Impact: MEDIUM (looks soft or noisy; fusion with LiDAR exposes floaters). Mitigation: gsplat MCMC with cap_max 2-3M, antialiased, opacity/scale regularization. Initialize from LiDAR (voxel-downsampled 1-3M points coloured from images) plus SfM points. Add confidence-masked LiDAR depth loss on ground and trunks. Cull floaters after training using the LiDAR free-space test: delete Gaussians whose centres are in voxels the LiDAR saw as empty along rays. CHEAP TEST: 2-min backyard 360 walk around one shrub and tree (no LiDAR). Train 7k steps at data_factor 2 in four variants: default vs MCMC vs absgrad vs delayed densification. Compare held-out PSNR/LPIPS, then visually inspect thin twigs. About 1 h total.** — Every forest GS paper reports these artifacts (TreeDGS, Sapling-NeRF, forest digital twins). LiDAR anchors are the best-documented fix. _Alternatives:_ 2DGS/RaDe-GS for crisper geometry (worse appearance). GaussianPlant-style structure primitives (research only, Dec 2025).
- **RISK R2 - Sky through the canopy: sky pixels explained by Gaussians at canopy depth, giving haze or floaters on upward views. Likelihood: HIGH. Impact: MEDIUM. Mitigation: sky segmentation masks with a separate background model (Splatfacto-W-style SH background, or a fixed far sphere of points). Exclude zenith virtual cameras from training, or downweight them. Never apply LiDAR depth loss on sky pixels. CHEAP TEST: same backyard data with and without sky masks and zenith views. Inspect a fly-through looking up at the canopy edge and count floaters.** — Splatfacto-W shows a background model with alpha loss cleans floaters. Under-canopy captures have large sky fractions in upward views. _Alternatives:_ Random background colour during training (gsplat random_bkgd) to discourage semi-transparent sky fits.
- **RISK R3 - Wind-moving foliage, giving ghosting and blurred canopy, and degrading SfM matches. Likelihood: MEDIUM (depends on the day; Swedish forest in autumn is often breezy). Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH for canopy, LOW for trunks and ground. Mitigation: schedule both runs on a forecast-calm, overcast day (subjective target: leaves barely moving). Capture Run 1 and Run 2 back-to-back. Delay densification (RobustSplat idea; a gsplat config change). Optionally use a residual-percentile robust mask after warm-up (Splatfacto-W style, easy to port into gsplat) or the SpotLessSplats fork (Apache-2.0). Do not use WildGaussians, RobustSplat or T-3DGS in deliverables (Inria NC). CHEAP TEST: synthetic Blender scene with a wind-animated tree (amplitude 0 / 2 / 5 cm), rendered as 360 video along a loop. Train the same config and measure the PSNR drop and robust-mask benefit with exact ground truth. Plus a 1-min real capture of one tree on a calm and on a breezy day.** — No production-grade wind solution exists (4D wind models appeared Sep 2026). Robust losses help with distractors (+1 to +6.5 dB) but may also discard fine static foliage. _Alternatives:_ Accept soft canopy and rely on LiDAR toggle for structure. Later research: 4DGS with oscillator priors (not for web delivery).
- **RISK R4 - Operator and BLK2GO visible in every 360 frame (Run 1: device above the camera; operator below or beside it). Likelihood: CERTAIN. Impact: HIGH if unmasked (operator 'ghost' smeared along the path and wrong SfM matches on a moving body). Mitigation: choose virtual-camera directions that avoid the nadir and handle zone. Generate per-view masks (SAM2 or a person/device segmenter, or a fixed mask, since the rig is fixed relative to the camera) and pass them to a trainer that honours masks (Brush mask folders, or a small gsplat loader patch). Mask the same regions during SfM feature extraction (COLMAP supports image masks). CHEAP TEST: 1-min capture in the backyard with the rig taped as planned. Render the planned virtual views and measure the fraction of pixels showing operator or device per view direction. Fix view directions and FOV from that.** — Practitioner guides state the operator is always in frame. The gsplat COLMAP loader ignores user masks by default (verified in colmap.py). _Alternatives:_ Monopod or raised mount for Run 2 (also improves viewpoint diversity).
- **RISK R5 - Exposure and WB variation (auto exposure, dappled sun) within a pass. Likelihood: HIGH (forest contrast is extreme). Impact: MEDIUM (flicker, colour blotches, floaters compensating brightness). Mitigation: lock ISO, shutter and WB on the Insta360 if the model allows, and capture overcast. Train with gsplat post_processing=ppisp, sharing per-frame parameters across all virtual views from the same 360 frame and lens. Freeze or disable vignetting for reprojected views, or train on dual fisheye where the vignetting model holds. Avoid per-frame bilateral grids (they overfit novel views). CHEAP TEST: backyard tree-line under partial sun, one auto-exposure and one locked-exposure capture. Train each with none / app_opt / bilateral grid / PPISP. Compare novel-view PSNR and visual flicker in a fly-through.** — PPISP beats BilaRF on novel views (24.62 vs 19.78 PSNR on T&T) and has day-0 gsplat support under Apache-2.0. _Alternatives:_ Inria-style per-image affine exposure (simplest); gsplat app_opt embeddings.
- **RISK R6 - Lighting and appearance change between Run 1 and Run 2 (sun or cloud change, different height and angle), causing cross-run SfM matching failure and double appearance. Likelihood: MEDIUM. Impact: MEDIUM. Mitigation: run back-to-back (under about 10 min apart), overcast. Use per-run PPISP or appearance groups. Register both runs in one SfM with sequential plus loop/vocab-tree matching. Fallback: register Run 2 separately to LiDAR via Sim(3) on stems and ground. CHEAP TEST: two backyard passes at 1.2 m and 2 m height, 10 min apart. Check that COLMAP or GLOMAP merges them into one component and what fraction of cross-run matches survive.** — The second viewpoint height is valuable for geometry, but only if the runs co-register. _Alternatives:_ Use the BLK2GO-registered Run 1 SfM as a fixed reference and localize Run 2 images into it (COLMAP image_registrator).
- **RISK R7 - Motion blur and rolling shutter in low under-canopy light. Likelihood: MEDIUM in Sweden in autumn, HIGH in the tropics. Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH (blurred frames produce floaters and SfM failures). Mitigation: walk at about 0.5 m/s, avoid fast yaw, and pick the sharpest frame per time window (Laplacian-variance or blur score) rather than a fixed fps. Use gsplat 3DGUT rolling-shutter support if needed. Accept higher ISO noise over long shutter (noise averages out in GS; blur does not). CHEAP TEST: 3 backyard walks at 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 m/s at dusk. Plot the sharpness score distribution and SfM registration rate. Choose the speed and frame-selection threshold from that.** — Guides insist on 1 m/s or less and slower in low light. 3DGUT added rolling-shutter modelling in gsplat 1.5.2. _Alternatives:_ Use the Insta360 photo/interval mode for stationary-stop captures in very dark tropical understory (much slower capture).
- **RISK R8 - Stitching seam and dual-lens parallax baked into equirect frames, giving systematic geometric error near the seam and on close objects. Likelihood: HIGH if training on stitched equirect. Impact: MEDIUM. Mitigation (DEVIATES from the user's Blender plan): treat the two raw fisheye lenses as a 2-camera rig (OPENCV_FISHEYE) in COLMAP and train fisheye directly in gsplat. Or, if using stitched equirect, generate overlapping perspective views with COLMAP's panorama_sfm (rig-aware) or a Python remap, keeping key views off the seam. Blender adds no value over a 30-line reprojection script except visualization. CHEAP TEST: put a checkerboard or fence 1-2 m from the camera on the seam line. Compare SfM reprojection error and splat sharpness for stitched-perspective vs raw-fisheye-rig on 30 s of footage.** — Practitioners say the seam is where distortion is worst. COLMAP itself says native spherical is less accurate than the rig-of-perspectives route. gsplat supports fisheye natively. _Alternatives:_ Seam360GS-style seam-aware training (ICCV 2025; code and license not verified).
- **RISK R9 - SfM fragmentation or failure under canopy (weak trunk texture, self-similar foliage, moving leaves, sky-dominated views). Likelihood: MEDIUM for a 10x10 m loop with 360 coverage, HIGH for long tropical walks. Impact: HIGH (blocks the whole pipeline). Mitigation: rig constraints across the virtual views of each 360 frame. Sequential matching plus loop detection. Try learned features (COLMAP 4.2 LoMa or ALIKED+LightGlue) vs SIFT. Use GLOMAP or the COLMAP global mapper. Design a fallback that bypasses SfM: camera poses from the time-synced BLK2GO trajectory composed with the lever-arm extrinsic, refined by gsplat pose_opt or COLMAP point triangulation with fixed poses. CHEAP TEST: first 60 s of the forest footage at 2 fps x 6 views. Run SIFT vs learned features, incremental vs global. Report registered-image %, number of components, mean track length. Under 1 h.** — TreeDGS needed a forest-trained matcher. COLMAP 4.1/4.2 (Jun-Sep 2026) added spherical models, rig fixes, gravity priors and learned features. _Alternatives:_ Pose priors from the LiDAR trajectory (COLMAP pose-prior mapper) to keep SfM metric and reduce failure modes.
- **RISK R10 - BLK2GO SLAM drift and degeneracy. Likelihood: LOW for 10x10 m with loop closure (expect about 1-5 cm). HIGH impact for 100 m+ or 1-ha tropical walks without closures. Mitigation: close the loop explicitly (start and end at the same marked spot, overlap the first 5 m). For long walks, plan figure-eight paths with several closures. Read the closure residuals reported by Cyclone. Place 3-4 fixed targets (stakes, spheres or checkerboards) with tape-measured distances as independent scale and drift checks. CHEAP TEST: walk the 10x10 m loop twice in one scan. Compare stem positions (fitted cylinders) between laps and the tape-measured target distances. Repeat a 100 m out-and-back walk without closure to measure open-loop drift per 100 m.** — Published BLK2GO residuals are 3.2-4.7 cm in tropical plots and outdoor accuracy is 10-60 mm. GNSS under canopy is biased by metres (6.38 m in one study), so GNSS is not an anchor. _Alternatives:_ Stem-feature graph optimization post hoc (Kukko-style) if drift appears in long tropical walks.
- **RISK R11 - LiDAR foliage noise (mixed pixels, wind-smeared leaves) biasing ICP or Sim(3) registration and depth supervision. Likelihood: HIGH presence. Impact: MEDIUM (cm-level bias and fuzzy depth targets). Mitigation: classify LiDAR into ground (CSF/PMF) plus stems (verticality and cylinder fitting) plus other. Register SfM or splat to LiDAR using only ground plus stems, with point-to-plane ICP and a robust kernel after Umeyama trajectory initialization. For depth loss, project only ground and stem points, apply a z-buffer or hidden-point-removal visibility filter per view, and use L1/Huber with confidence weights. Filter statistical outliers and isolated points first. CHEAP TEST (synthetic, no field work): take the BLK2GO cloud, sample a pseudo-SfM cloud, apply a known Sim(3) (scale 0.37, 20 deg rotation, 3 m translation) plus foliage noise. Compare recovered vs true transforms using all points vs ground plus stems only. Report translation, rotation and scale errors.** — Foliage is the least stable geometry for both sensors. Trunks and ground are rigid and well observed by both. _Alternatives:_ Register using rendered splat depth maps vs LiDAR (dense) instead of SfM sparse points.
- **RISK R12 - Time synchronization: the clap is unusable because the BLK2GO records no audio. Likelihood that clap-only sync fails: CERTAIN. Impact: HIGH for trajectory-based extrinsic estimation (1 frame = 33 ms gives 1.7-3.3 cm and 1-3 deg error at 0.5-1 m/s and 30-90 deg/s). Mitigation (DEVIATES from user plan): start and end each recording with the rig held still for 3 s, then 3-4 sharp yaw-pitch-roll wiggles, then still for 3 s. Estimate the offset by cross-correlating angular-velocity magnitude from SfM camera rotations (scale-free) against the BLK2GO trajectory. Refine to sub-frame by interpolation. Estimate offset at the start and end to detect clock skew. Keep the clap only for multi-camera audio sync if a second camera is added. CHEAP TEST: 2-min backyard capture with both devices, wiggles at start and end. Verify the estimated offset is consistent at start vs end (within 1 frame) and stable across 3 repeats.** — Rotation rate is observable by both sensors, needs no scale, and gives sub-frame precision. Insta360 X-series gyro extraction through open tools is unconfirmed. _Alternatives:_ Insta360 gyro from .insv via telemetry-parser, if X-series is supported (unverified); a visible LED or flash event if the BLK2GO imagery and timestamps are exportable.
- **RISK R13 - Lever arm and extrinsic between BLK2GO and Insta360 poorly observable or not constant (tape flex). Likelihood: MEDIUM. Impact: MEDIUM (disagreement between measured and estimated offset, misaligned overlay). Mitigation: rigid clamp even for the first test if possible. Include rotation excitation about at least 2 non-parallel axes (hand-eye calibration requires it, otherwise translation components are unobservable). Estimate the extrinsic in sliding 20-30 s windows and report its variance; a flexing mount shows as drift. Compare against the tape-measured prior with stated tolerances (for example within 1 cm and 1-2 deg). CHEAP TEST: in the backyard, record 2 min with deliberate pitch and roll wiggles plus 2 min of straight walking only. Show that the straight-walk-only estimate has large uncertainty in vertical lever arm while the wiggle data constrains it.** — Standard hand-eye calibration theory (background knowledge). The user explicitly wants measured-vs-estimated agreement reporting. _Alternatives:_ Direct registration of each 360 frame's local SfM points to LiDAR (point-cloud-based extrinsic) as a second independent estimate.
- **RISK R14 - VRAM OOM on the 16 GB laptop GPU. Likelihood: MEDIUM for 10x10 m if using default densification at near-full 360-derived resolution. HIGH for tropical scenes. Impact: MEDIUM (crashes, forced quality cuts). Mitigation: MCMC strategy with cap_max 2-4M, training views about 1.5-2 MP (for example 1600-1920 px squares), packed=True, absgrad, test at 7k steps first. Keep images on CPU (default DataLoader; do not use cache_images unless RAM allows: about 3,000 x 3.7 MP x 3 bytes = about 33 GB). Log torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated per 1k steps. CHEAP TEST: on the first real dataset, sweep cap_max {1M, 2M, 4M} x resolution {1024, 1600, 2048} for 3k steps each. Fit a GB-per-million-Gaussians-per-MP curve and use it to size tiles for later scenes.** — gsplat eval shows about 2 / 3.4 / 5 GB for 1M / 2M / 3M Gaussians (MCMC) at Mip-NeRF360 resolution. The user's GPU has 16 GB GDDR6, with WDDM/display overhead on top. _Alternatives:_ LichtFeld Studio (GPLv3, Windows-native, MCMC) or Brush (Apache-2.0) if gsplat Windows builds are painful; CLM-style offload only for non-commercial evaluation.
- **RISK R15 - License contamination from research code (Inria NC or CC-NC licenses in WildGaussians, RobustSplat, T-3DGS, CLM, CityGS, Hierarchical 3DGS, LoD-of-Gaussians). Likelihood: HIGH if chasing SOTA. Impact: HIGH (Hexagon is a commercial entity; the outputs are shared publicly). Mitigation: whitelist gsplat, PPISP, 3DGRUT, Brush, Spark (Apache/MIT), LichtFeld (GPLv3, fine for local tool use), SpotLessSplats fork, Splatfacto-W, DeSplat. Use NC code only for side-by-side evaluation, and confirm with Hexagon legal whether internal R&D counts as 'non-commercial'. CHEAP TEST: a license-audit script over the Python environment (pip-licenses) plus a manual check of any CUDA rasterizer submodule (diff-gaussian-rasterization is Inria NC).** — Verified LICENSE files for WildGaussians and RobustSplat (Inria NC), CityGS (CC BY-NC-SA), CLM (Inria NC), LoD-of-Gaussians (CC BY-NC-ND). _Alternatives:_ Implement needed ideas (delayed densification, residual masks, tiling) directly on gsplat.
- **RISK R16 - Phone viewer cannot handle the splat count. Likelihood: MEDIUM. Impact: MEDIUM. Mitigation: train the 10x10 m scene to 1-1.5M splats for a phone-safe version (Spark default LoD budgets are 1M on Android and 1.5M on iOS). Produce a desktop version at 3-5M. For anything bigger, use Spark build-lod --quality with --rad-chunked and paged loading. CHEAP TEST: export the backyard splat at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4M. Measure fps on a mid-range Android and an iPhone in a minimal Three.js + Spark page.** — Spark 2.3.1 (Oct 2026) documents these budgets and LoD streaming. _Alternatives:_ Compress with SOG/SPZ for download size; LuciadRIA as an optional second viewer only.
- **RISK R17 - Ground detection and navigation boundaries in vegetation: low shrubs or ferns classified as ground, or holes where ground is unseen. Likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM in Swedish pine/spruce forest, HIGH in tropical understory. Impact: MEDIUM (camera clipping or falling through). Mitigation: CSF ground filter on LiDAR, then a gridded DTM (10-25 cm) with hole filling (inpainting or TIN). Use a minimum eye height above the DTM plus collision proxies from stem cylinders. CHEAP TEST: run CSF on the first BLK2GO scan. Visually check a DTM hillshade for bumps from ferns and stumps, and measure the hole fraction.** — Navigation constraints come from LiDAR ground; vegetation contamination is the classic DTM failure under understory (background knowledge). _Alternatives:_ PMF/progressive TIN densification; semantic segmentation of LiDAR (for example ForestFormer3D, used by TreeDGS).
- **TROPICAL DESIGN-IN (do it from the start even for the 10x10 m test): (a) poses from the LiDAR trajectory plus extrinsic as priors, with SfM as refinement only. (b) Spatial tiling on gsplat: 15-25 m tiles from the LiDAR ground footprint with 3-5 m overlap; select cameras by frustum overlap; train each tile with its own cap; crop to the tile core; merge; then build Spark LoD. (c) LiDAR initialization plus confidence-masked ground/stem depth loss. (d) PPISP grouped per 360 frame. (e) Capture SOP covering lens fogging (acclimatize the camera 20-30 min in a sealed bag), lens cleaning, battery and walk-length planning, and closed-loop path design. (f) Store all timestamps, extrinsics and transforms in one metric project frame (LiDAR) from day 1. CHEAP TEST: run the tiling code on the 10x10 m scene split into 4 tiles of 6x6 m. Compare against the single-model training for seams, PSNR and Gaussian count. This validates chunking before it is needed.** — Tropical 1-ha handheld scans take about 40 min (verified study), meaning tens of thousands of views and tens of millions of Gaussians. Permissive chunked trainers do not exist, and SfM is more fragile in dense understory. _Alternatives:_ Evaluate CLM or Hierarchical 3DGS on the same data for quality reference only (NC).

## risks

- R2 Sky through canopy fitted as near geometry. Likelihood HIGH, impact MEDIUM. Test: with vs without sky masks and zenith views.

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The 19-risk register and its cheap-test structure mostly hold up. The core technical facts checked out against primary sources: gsplat memory numbers, COLMAP 4.1/4.2 features, the PPISP results, Spark budgets, and most LICENSE files. The issues are a few wrong facts and several 'easy' steps that are not easy. Changes I would make:

(1) R8 (raw fisheye): not off-the-shelf. gsplat's COLMAP loader undistorts fisheye to cropped pinhole images. Native >180 deg training needs FTheta + 3DGUT, which is main-only, MCMC-only and needs a calibrated max_angle. For v1, use COLMAP rig-of-perspective views (panorama_sfm or a Python remap). Treat native fisheye, or the NCore v4 path, as a later experiment. Dropping Blender is still right.

(2) R12 (time sync): make the primary method the Insta360 gyro read with telemetry-parser (it handles X4/X5), cross-correlated with BLK2GO trajectory angular rate. Keep SfM-rotation correlation as the independent check. Prefer an X4 or X5 over an X3. Confirming that Cyclone exports a timestamped BLK2GO trajectory is still the top blocking question.

(3) R5 (lighting): PPISP still beats bilateral grids, but always include an uncompensated baseline, because BilaRF and controller-less PPISP scored below it on novel views. Reduce ppisp_controller_activation_num_steps (default 25k) for the 3k-7k-step tests, and budget custom code to share per-frame parameters per 360 frame.

(4) R9 (SfM): replace GLOMAP with the COLMAP 4.x global mapper, since GLOMAP was archived on 2026-03-09. Run SIFT vs ALIKED+LightGlue vs LoMa inside COLMAP 4.2.1. Downgrade the TreeDGS-based prior: that is aerial evidence, not under-canopy.

(5) R10 (drift): remove the 3.2-4.7 cm 'BLK2GO tropical residual'; the study does not name the device or report residuals. Rely on the double-lap and target test.

(6) R4 and R11: gsplat already consumes masks the dataset supplies. Consider writing the data as NCore v4 (Apache-2.0) to get static rig/ego masks, LiDAR init, fisheye/FTheta rigs and START/END poses without forking gsplat. Keep the confidence-masked ground/stem depth loss as custom code.

(7) R14/R18: enable packed by default; its speed cost is about 6-9%, not 15-20%, and on large scenes it saves 46-50%. Plan a source build of gsplat main + PPISP on Windows on day 1, because the 1.5.3 wheels lack the 2026 features. The 4-6M Gaussian cap on 16 GB is optimistic for foliage at about 3.7 MP views; keep the measurement sweep.

(8) R15 (licenses): the Inria license expressly allows research and evaluation by industrial research users, so NC Inria-based tools (WildGaussians, RobustSplat, CLM, H3DGS) can be benchmarked internally but not shipped. CC BY-NC-SA (CityGS) needs legal review. 'A LoD of Gaussians' has no public code license. LichtFeld is free only as a source build; its Windows binaries are paid.

(9) Drop the uncited '20-30% LiDAR fusion gain' and the '≤1 m/s' walking speed from the rationale, and derive walking speed from the R7 test.

(10) Fix the 3DGRUT version (v1.1.0, 2026-06-10) and note that Spark's 30M figure applies to in-browser LoD building.

The tropical design-in items survive: LiDAR pose priors, permissive tiling on gsplat, LiDAR init plus a masked depth loss, LoD export, and the capture SOP. Add KHR_gaussian_splatting (ratified 2026) as an interoperability export target next to Spark RAD/SPZ for a later Luciad path.
