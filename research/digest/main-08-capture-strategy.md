# capture-strategy

## Summary

CAPTURE STRATEGY VERDICT (research cut-off 2026-10-06; note: the shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through, so later checks used WebFetch on known URLs only, and some numbers below are my own derivations, marked as such). Your plan (Run 1 = BLK2GO + Insta360 around the 10x10 m square; Run 2 = Insta360 only, held higher at a different angle) is a reasonable start, but it has five gaps that will hurt the alignment goal and the splat itself. (1) Run 2 without LiDAR loses metric pose priors for half the images and a second LiDAR pass (Leica says two passes improve coverage). (2) A perimeter-only path leaves the plot interior seen only from its edges, so the viewer will show holes and floaters there. (3) "Different angle" makes little difference for a 360 camera, since it sees every direction anyway. What matters is a different height and a sideways offset. Tilting only moves the stitch seam. (4) Walking a loop mostly rotates the rig around the vertical (yaw) axis. Hand-eye/trajectory calibration needs at least 2 motions with non-parallel rotation axes (OpenCV). With yaw-only motion, the offset along the vertical axis cannot be recovered, and that is exactly the main component of a camera mounted below the BLK2GO. So the software cannot confirm your measured vertical offset unless you add deliberate pitch and roll motions. (5) Tape flexes, so the extrinsic is not rigid. Also, a camera under the base probably prevents the required static start on the BLK2GO table stand.

RECOMMENDED PROTOCOL. Keep the BLK2GO scanning for every camera pass. Do all passes in ONE continuous scan of about 8-12 min, inside Leica partner guidance of 10-15 min walks and the 45-50 min battery, starting and ending at the same staked point for loop closure. Passes: A) perimeter loop counter-clockwise at about 1.1-1.2 m camera height (rig held in front at chest height), on the square edge. B) perimeter loop clockwise at about 2.0-2.2 m (rig held overhead like a torch, or on a pole), offset 1.5-2 m outside the edge so edge trees are seen from both sides. C) interior coverage at about 1.5 m: a '#' grid (lines at about 3.3 m and 6.7 m in both directions) or a figure-8 through the centre, keeping ≥1 m from trunks. Optional D) a low loop at about 0.7-0.9 m for ground and understory detail. Optional E) a camera-only pole pass at 2.5-3 m, as an add-on and not a replacement. Practitioner guidance agrees on 3 passes at hip, shoulder and elevated heights (3dvf 2026), perimeter plus figure-8 or inner loop (FreeGaussian 2026), ≥3 passes at different heights (Lahitte 2026), and closed loops (Immersal, Splatware). Walk at 0.3-0.5 m/s (guides say ≤1 m/s; forest is close and complex). Turn slowly, and pause 5-10 s at corners and between passes (Leica). Stay ≥1 m from objects: BLK2GO minimum range is 0.5 m horizontal and 0.7 m vertical, and the Insta360 manual warns against subjects close to the side of the camera (seam).

START/END RITUAL (both ends): start the Insta360 recording first. Initialize the BLK2GO static (table stand or tripod) for 10-15 s until the LED is green, then wait 10 more s. Clap 3 times near the camera. Do 3 sharp-but-controlled yaw twists for motion-based sync, since the BLK2GO has no audio and Gyroflow does not support Insta360 360 cameras. Then a 30 s 'calibration dance' of pitch ±20-30° and roll ±20-30°, 3 cycles each, plus yaw. Repeating at the end lets you estimate clock drift. A bonus: the Insta360 can see the BLK2GO LED ring change state, which gives a visual sync marker.

CAMERA SETTINGS (X5; the X6 shipped 2026-08-12 with larger 1/1.1-inch sensors and 8K50, which helps under canopy). Use 360 Video mode (not PureVideo or InstaFrame, which allow only auto exposure) at 8K30. Use Manual exposure (the X5 manual confirms Shutter, ISO, WB and I-Log are settable in 360 Video manual mode). Set shutter 1/500 (1/250 minimum in dim canopy, 1/1000 if bright). Fix ISO: expect about 400-1600 under an overcast canopy (my derivation at f/2.0). Lock WB (about 5500 K). Turn Active HDR, AdaptiveTone and Balanced Exposure off. Use Standard profile (I-Log only if you grade consistently), low/medium sharpness, high bitrate, H.265. Remove the lens guards and clean both lenses. Export without FlowState stabilization or direction lock, or use the raw dual-fisheye. Stabilization breaks the rigid camera-to-BLK2GO rotation you want to verify.

LIGHT/WEATHER. Choose uniform overcast at midday (about 10:00-14:00). Sun elevation at noon in early October at 59-63°N is only about 22-27° (derived), so sunny days give long, high-contrast sunflecks. Passing clouds are worse than steady sun. Pick calm days and wait for still foliage. Moving branches cause floaters in the splat and ghost points in the LiDAR (ForestSplats/wild-GS literature). The BLK2GO is rated only +5 to +40 °C, a real limit in a Swedish October. Let the gear acclimatize in a bag to avoid condensation (Insta360 manual). Do all passes within about 1 h on the same day.

RIG. Replace the tape with a rigid bracket, ideally the REALiT OmniGO handle clamp ($695: 5/8-inch survey thread, ARCA, accessory points meant for 360 cameras) or a machined or printed collar. Keep the camera inside the BLK2GO's downward blind cone. Its 270° vertical FOV implies roughly a 45° half-angle cone below the device, and Leica itself says to keep the phone 'below or behind'. Keep everything away from the dome and the 3 panoramic SLAM camera windows. Mount the Insta360 coaxially beneath the BLK2GO, inverted is fine. Point the lenses left and right relative to the walking direction, so the seam faces forward and back: sideways views carry the most parallax, and the operator sits in the rear seam and gets masked anyway. Witness-mark the rig, photograph it, and re-measure the offset before and after each session. On the BLK2GO, enable Store Images, uncompressed images and full density (Immersal's BLK2GO workflow), so its posed panoramic images can serve as an extra cross-modal check.

GROUND TRUTH. Set 5 control stations (4 corners about 0.5 m outside the path, plus the centre). Each gets a stake with a 200 mm matte white sphere at about 1.2-1.5 m (for LiDAR sphere fitting) and a rigid matte AprilTag tag36h11 board with a 30-40 cm black square facing the plot (A2 print). Add 2-3 boards at other heights (ground at the centre, about 2 m on a trunk) so the vertical is constrained, and one or two 1.000 m scale bars with tags at both ends. Tape-measure the 4 sides and 2 diagonals between stakes. Split the targets into control and held-out check sets.

OPERATOR. The camera sees the operator in every frame, so plan to mask. Wear dark, plain clothing, but hi-vis may be needed: moose hunting season in Sweden typically starts in October (check locally; hi-vis can be masked). Holding the rig overhead puts the operator in the nadir. A second person keeps the log, does the claps, watches for intruders, and stands still well outside the plot during passes. Leica: nobody walks directly in front of or behind the scanner.

DATA VOLUME (estimate): about 150-200 m of path at 0.4 m/s gives 7-9 min of 8K30 video. Sampling every 0.25-0.3 m gives about 550-800 equirect frames, which become about 1.5-4k perspective crops after culling blur. That is plausible for 16 GB VRAM at about 1.6k px; the processing planner should confirm. Insta360 Spatial Capture is cloud processing (Remy Innovation, Shenzhen), so it is excluded by your local-only requirement.

## Recommendations

- **Replace 'Run 2 = camera only' with LiDAR running for all camera passes, ideally ONE continuous BLK2GO scan of about 8-12 min containing every pass, started and ended at the same staked point. Keep a camera-only pole pass only as an optional add-on (Run E).** — Every Insta360 frame then gets a metric BLK2GO pose prior in one SLAM frame (no LiDAR-to-LiDAR registration needed). Leica says two passes improve LiDAR coverage. It also roughly doubles the data for estimating and checking the extrinsic. The duration fits partner guidance of 10-15 min walks and the 45-50 min battery. _Alternatives:_ Two separate scans (one per run) with ≥30% overlap and shared targets, registered afterwards. A camera-only Run 2 on a 3 m Invisible Selfie Stick registered via joint SfM with Run 1 (works, but has no pose priors and gives no second extrinsic estimate).
- **Pass design for the 10x10 m plot (minimum 3, ideal 4-5). A: perimeter, counter-clockwise, camera about 1.1-1.2 m (rig in front at chest height), on the edge. B: perimeter, clockwise, camera about 2.0-2.2 m (rig held overhead or on a pole), offset 1.5-2 m OUTSIDE the edge. C: interior '#' grid (lines at about 3.3 and 6.7 m both ways) or a figure-8 through the centre at about 1.5 m, ≥1 m from trunks, turning outside the plot. D (optional): low loop at 0.7-0.9 m for ground/understory. E (optional): camera-only 2.5-3 m pole loop. Pause 5-10 s at corners and between passes; overlap the start and end of each loop by about 2 m.** — Perimeter-only capture leaves the interior (where viewers will fly) under-sampled, and edge trees one-sided. Multiple heights add vertical parallax and cover the 1.2-2.0 m eye heights the viewer will use. Opposite directions and offsets diversify viewpoints. Path about 150-200 m, about 6-9 min at 0.4 m/s. _Alternatives:_ Lawnmower with 2.5 m spacing if the understory is sparse; a spiral inward from the perimeter if trunks block straight lines.
- **Walking and handling: 0.3-0.5 m/s (never >1 m/s), slow turns (aim ≤30°/s), constant height within each pass, ≥1 m (ideally 1.5-4 m) from trunks and branches, and no sudden accelerations. Hold the 360 camera level (vertical axis); do not tilt it for 'different angles'. Change height and lateral offset instead.** — The BLK2GO needs a slow constant pace and ≥1 m distance (minimum range 0.5 m H / 0.7 m V). The Insta360 seam fails on close objects. Motion blur and rolling-shutter skew grow with rotation rate. Tilting a 360 camera only moves the seam and blind zones. _Alternatives:_ A slightly faster pace (0.6-0.8 m/s) is acceptable in bright light at 1/1000 s.
- **Start/end ritual at the staked start point (do it at BOTH start and end): (1) start Insta360 recording; (2) initialize the BLK2GO fully static (table stand or OmniGO on tripod) for 10-15 s until the LED is green, then wait 10 s more, with the camera seeing the LED ring; (3) clap 3 times about 0.5 m from the camera; (4) 3 crisp yaw twists of about ±45° at about 90°/s; (5) 30 s calibration dance of pitch ±20-30° x3, roll ±20-30° x3, yaw ±45° x3, slowly (about 2 s per cycle) while standing still; (6) walk. At the end, repeat 3-5, then set the rig static and stop the BLK2GO, then the camera.** — The BLK2GO has no audio, so the clap alone cannot sync it. Rotation events let software cross-correlate SfM camera rotation with the BLK2GO trajectory, and events at both ends estimate clock drift. Pitch/roll excitation is needed for the vertical extrinsic component (OpenCV: ≥2 non-parallel rotation axes). _Alternatives:_ A visual flash (phone screen strobe) seen by both the camera and the BLK2GO panoramic cameras, if BLK2GO images are stored.
- **Rig: drop tape before the first serious test. Use OmniGO ($695) or a rigid machined/3D-printed collar on the handle with a 1/4"-20 screw and an anti-twist pin. Mount the Insta360 coaxially BELOW the BLK2GO (inverted is fine), entirely inside the downward cone of about 45° half-angle under the LiDAR, clear of the dome and the 3 panoramic camera windows, the battery door and the buttons. Set the lenses to face left/right relative to the walking direction (seam fore/aft). Witness-mark, photograph, and re-measure the offset (steel rule/caliper ±2 mm, angles ±2-3°) before and after each session. Record the reference points used (e.g., BLK2GO dome-top centre or base centre; Insta360 midpoint between the lens vertices).** — Tape flex makes the extrinsic time-varying and invalidates the measured-vs-estimated comparison. The blind-cone placement avoids LiDAR occlusion (Leica: phone 'below or behind'). Sideways lens centres capture the high-parallax directions, and the operator lands in the masked rear seam. A camera under the base likely blocks the table-stand initialization, which OmniGO plus a tripod solves. _Alternatives:_ Pole rig: OmniGO on a 5/8" survey pole (Scan&Go 3.6 m, 1.5 kg load) with the Insta360 on a short side arm 25-35 m below... corrected: 25-35 cm below the BLK2GO and ≤15 cm off-axis (stays inside the blind cone). It gives higher passes with less arm fatigue.
- **Insta360 settings: 360 Video mode (not PureVideo/InstaFrame/Timelapse), 8K30 (X6: 8K30 or 8K50). Manual exposure: shutter 1/500 (floor 1/250 under dark canopy; 1/1000 if bright); ISO fixed at the lowest value that exposes trunks and ground correctly (expect 400-1600 under an overcast canopy), exposing for shaded mid-tones and letting sky gaps clip. WB fixed (about 5500 K overcast; shoot a grey card at the start). Active HDR, AdaptiveTone and Balanced Exposure off. Standard colour (I-Log only with consistent grading), sharpness low/medium, bitrate High, H.265. Lens guards removed. Do a 30 s test clip and review it on site. In post: export without FlowState/direction lock, or use raw dual-fisheye.** — Exposure/WB lock is the dominant splat guidance (flicker destabilizes training). Manual mode gives identical, time-invariant exposure on both lenses, whereas auto balancing varies per frame. Stabilization rotates frames independently of the rig, breaking the rigid extrinsic. High bitrate limits H.265 smearing of foliage. _Alternatives:_ If the canopy is very dark: 1/250 s plus ISO ≤3200 and slower walking (0.3 m/s). 3dvf suggests AdaptiveTone for high-contrast scenes; test it only as a secondary clip.
- **Weather/time window: stable uniform overcast, midday (about 10:00-14:00 in Swedish October), dry (no drizzle; droplets on the lenses ruin frames), calm wind (foliage visibly still; aim for forecasts of about ≤2-3 m/s), air temperature ≥5 °C for the BLK2GO. Capture all passes within about 1 h. Acclimatize the gear in a closed bag for 15-30 min to avoid condensation.** — Overcast removes moving sunflecks and extreme dynamic range; low October sun (about 22-27° at noon, derived) makes dappled light harsh. Passing clouds cause exposure jumps under locked exposure. Wind creates floaters and LiDAR ghosts. The BLK2GO spec is +5 to +40 °C, and the Insta360 manual warns about condensation. _Alternatives:_ If only sunny days are available: shoot fast, near midday, with consistent exposure set for shade. For tropical forests later: early morning for calm air, but watch for fog, lens fogging and the 40 °C camera limit at 8K.
- **Ground truth: 5 stations (4 corners about 0.5 m outside the path, plus the centre), each a stake with a 200 mm matte white/grey sphere at 1.2-1.5 m and a rigid matte AprilTag tag36h11 board (A2, black square 30-40 cm, unique IDs) facing the plot centre at a measured offset from the sphere. Add 2-3 tag boards at other heights (flat on the ground at the centre, about 2 m on a trunk) and 1-2 scale bars of exactly 1.000 m with tags at both ends (one horizontal, one vertical/diagonal). Tape the 4 sides and 2 diagonals between sphere stakes. Use 3-4 stations as control and the rest as check.** — A similarity transform needs ≥3 non-collinear points; 5-8 targets give redundancy and independent checks. Spheres suit noisy SLAM point clouds; tags give sub-pixel image corners; the scale bars validate scale independently of the LiDAR. Non-coplanar heights constrain tilt/vertical. _Alternatives:_ Large black/white checkerboards (A2/A1) detectable in both LiDAR intensity and images; natural features (trunk centres at 1.3 m) as additional, lower-accuracy checks.
- **People: operator in dark, plain, matte clothing (add hi-vis if the hunting season is active; it will be masked), keeping the same body position relative to the rig throughout. A second person holds the field log/timer, does claps, keeps others out, and stands still ≥5 m outside the plot (ideally behind a large trunk) during passes, never walking in front of or behind the operator. Plan to mask the operator (SAM2/SAM3-based tools) in all frames.** — A 360 camera always sees the operator; holding the rig overhead pushes the operator into the nadir. Leica warns that people near the scanner block the LiDAR/cameras and confuse SLAM. A still bystander is easier to mask and clean than a moving one. _Alternatives:_ A solo capture works if the BLK2GO Live app is not used (one-button operation) and the log is kept by voice notes on the Insta360 audio.
- **BLK2GO configuration: enable Store Images, uncompressed images and full density. Keep the phone (if the app is used) below or behind the device. Check the loop-closure indication in BLK2GO Live before leaving the site.** — This gives globally-shuttered, SLAM-posed panoramic images in the LiDAR frame for cross-modal registration/checks, and the densest cloud for ground detection and targets. _Alternatives:_ Default settings if storage is limited; then rely on targets plus trajectory alignment only.
- **Frame budget for processing (estimate for the planner): sample equirect frames every 0.25-0.3 m along the path (about every 15-25th frame at 30 fps at 0.4 m/s), drop blurred frames and the sync/calibration-dance frames from training (keep them for calibration), which gives about 550-800 frames, or about 1.5-4k perspective crops at about 1.6k px.** — Video gives high overlap; denser sampling adds compute without new parallax. Matches guidance of about 2 fps / 0.5 s intervals and keeps within 16 GB VRAM training budgets. _Alternatives:_ Use all frames for SfM tracking but a subset for 3DGS training.
- **FIELD CHECKLIST. BEFORE: forecast (overcast, calm, ≥5 °C, dry); charge BLK2GO battery(ies), Insta360 battery x2, phone; empty microSD (V30, ≥256 GB) plus BLK2GO storage; firmware updated at home; clean lenses and dome; remove lens guards; rigid mount tightened, witness-marked, photographed, offset measured; stakes, flagging, sphere targets, A2 tag boards, scale bars, tape measure, mallet, grey card, lens cloth/blower, tripod or table stand, gaffer and zip-ties as backup, field log sheet; mark the plot corners, centre and start point; place and photograph targets; tape the sides and diagonals; walk the path once without recording to clear trip hazards; preset the camera (360 Video 8K30, manual 1/500, ISO, WB, HDR off, high bitrate) and record a 30 s test clip to check exposure/blur on screen. DURING: camera recording, then BLK2GO static init (LED green, then +10 s); claps x3, yaw twists x3, pitch/roll dance 30 s; passes A, B, C (D, E optional) at 0.3-0.5 m/s with 5-10 s corner pauses; watch the BLK2GO LED (yellow = slow down, orange = stop); helper logs times, heights, incidents and wind gusts; at the end, ritual repeated at the start point, rig static, stop BLK2GO, stop camera. AFTER: check the BLK2GO Live loop closure and preview; spot-check camera footage for blur/exposure/lens drops; verify each target is visible at close range; re-measure and photograph the rig offset; log temperature, cloud, wind and times; copy both datasets to two drives before leaving; leave the stakes if a repeat visit is planned.** — Combines Leica BLK2GO practice, Insta360 manual guidance and splat capture guides into one repeatable procedure. _Alternatives:_ A reduced checklist for quick tests: skip spheres; keep the 5 tag boards plus 1 scale bar and the start/end ritual.

## risks

- BLK2GO is rated +5 to +40 °C; Swedish October mornings can fall below 5 °C. Capture at midday and keep the unit warm before use.
- Trajectory-based extrinsic estimation cannot recover the vertical offset from walking (yaw-only) motion. Without the pitch/roll calibration dance, the software cannot confirm the measured offset of a camera mounted directly below.
- A taped mount flexes and creeps, so the extrinsic varies over time. This invalidates the rigid-offset comparison and can corrupt pose priors.
- A camera attached under the BLK2GO base may prevent the required static table-stand initialization. A handheld init risks SLAM quality.
- With the rig at chest height, the operator fills roughly 10-15% of the sphere at 0.4-0.6 m (derived estimate), so large masks are needed. Holding the rig overhead reduces this.
- Low light under canopy forces a trade-off between noise (high ISO) and blur (slow shutter); rolling-shutter skew appears on fast turns.
- Wind-driven foliage and passing clouds produce floaters, inconsistent appearance and LiDAR ghost points. Falling autumn leaves add transients.
- Insta360 stabilization or direction-lock exports decouple frame orientation from the rig and silently break extrinsic estimation.
- Auto exposure features (AdaptiveTone, Balanced Exposure, Active HDR) vary per frame and between lenses, causing brightness flicker that destabilizes training.
- Dual-fisheye seam parallax (about cm-level) creates ghosting on close objects. Subjects closer than about 1 m near the seam are unreliable.
- H.265 compression smears high-frequency foliage; standard bitrate may lose SfM features.
- BLK2GO SLAM noise/'spaghetti' drift outdoors (10-60 mm). Without loop closure and check targets, the LiDAR reference itself may be off by cm.
- Condensation or lens fogging moving from a warm car to cold, humid forest (and later in the tropics); droplets on the lenses ruin segments.
- Safety: October is moose-hunting season in much of Sweden (background knowledge, verify locally). Wear hi-vis and inform landowners and hunting teams.
- Datasets that are too large (thousands of 8K-derived crops) may exceed practical 16 GB VRAM training budgets unless subsampled.

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The recommendations mostly hold, with some changes. Note: the shared WebSearch budget was already used up when this check started, so every check used WebFetch on known primary URLs, and some items stay unverifiable (MDPI and the Insta360 FAQ returned 403).

What holds:
- Keep the BLK2GO running for every camera pass, in one continuous scan of about 8-12 min that starts and ends at the same staked point. This fits Leica's two-pass and same-start/end guidance and the partner's 10-15 min walks.
- Use several heights plus interior crossing paths instead of a perimeter-only route.
- Use X5/X6 360 Video mode in Manual (the manual confirms Shutter/ISO/WB are manual only there; PureVideo and InstaFrame are auto-only), with a shutter of about 1/500.
- Prefer overcast, calm, midday conditions, respecting the BLK2GO's +5 °C limit.
- Use a rigid mount (OmniGO confirmed at $695, with 360-camera accessory points).
- Use spheres plus AprilTag targets (sizes supported by the TLS literature).

What I would change:
1. Downgrade the claim that the vertical offset is unobservable. It is true only for trajectory-only (AX=XB) estimation. Registering SfM to LiDAR via point cloud and targets observes the full offset directly. Keep a gentle pitch/roll sequence as insurance, not as a requirement.
2. Rebuild time sync around X5 gyro telemetry via telemetry-parser (open, MIT/Apache, X5 supported), cross-correlated with BLK2GO trajectory angular velocity. Drop the sharp ~90°/s twists, because Leica asks for smooth motion; use moderate rotations instead. The clap does nothing for the BLK2GO. If the X6 is chosen, first check that telemetry-parser supports it, or use the X5 for the first test.
3. Do not hold the rig in front of the chest for Pass A. Leica says this blocks the LiDAR's view behind you. Get low camera views with the rig held out to the side or on a short pole, and keep the BLK2GO high for the other passes.
4. Drop the idea that the camera can see the LED ring with a coaxial-below mount, and drop BLK2GO stored images as a pipeline input (proprietary .b2g, Immersal cloud only). Enable Store Images only as an inexpensive just-in-case.
5. Treat seam fore/aft as a judgment call that goes against Insta360's own tip, not a rule. Its value depends on whether processing uses stitched equirect crops or raw fisheye / COLMAP spherical or rig models. COLMAP 4.1+ now offers native spherical cameras as an open alternative to Blender virtual cameras.
6. Mount the camera on OmniGO accessory points and use the OmniGO's flat underside as the init stand. This resolves the table-stand conflict.
7. Use the same X5 lens set with no lens guards in every session, because lenses are swappable and swapping changes the calibration.
8. Correct the safety note: northern Sweden's moose season is already open, and southern Sweden's opens 8 October.
9. tag36h11 is fine because OpenCV supports it, but note that AprilTag itself recommends tagStandard41h12. The px-per-cell estimate is conservative, so A3 boards may already be enough at 3-4 m.

The frame budget (about 600-720 equirect frames over a 150-200 m path) and the 0.3-0.5 m/s pace remain reasonable.
