# drone-alignment

## Summary

TOPIC: how to register drone imagery, or a model built from it, to a BLK2GO scan (local metric frame, no GNSS) and to the Insta360 ground splat. Also covers what this means for the shared coordinate frame and for georeferencing.

BOTTOM LINE: The most reliable way to register a drone model to an under-canopy handheld LiDAR scan is physical. Use shared targets (GCPs) that the drone sees from above and the BLK2GO scans in the same walk. Put them in a clearing, on a forest road or at the forest edge. Use them as control points in the drone bundle adjustment (BA), with coordinates measured directly in the LiDAR cloud. Expected drone-to-LiDAR accuracy is about 2-5 cm at the targets. Drone RTK/PPK is a big help: metric scale, near-cm horizontal placement, fewer GCPs needed, optional world placement. It does not remove the need for shared targets, because the BLK2GO frame has no GNSS. Three other methods should NOT be the primary one, only refinements or fallbacks: marker-free geometric registration (canopy/stems ICP), joint aerial-ground SfM with the Insta360 frames, and consumer-drone GPS alone.

WHY GEOMETRY OVERLAP IS POOR: The BLK2GO has 0.5-25 m range. From the ground it sees the forest floor, stems and the underside of the lower canopy. Drone photogrammetry sees the top of the canopy plus open ground only. UAV-SfM "cannot penetrate the vegetation to reach the ground", and it misses small gaps or overestimates canopy height in them (tropical study, Guyana). So under closed canopy the two clouds show different sides of the crowns, and ICP on them is biased or fails. Overlap really exists only in clearings, on tracks and at forest edges. Even LiDAR-to-LiDAR marker-free aerial-terrestrial registration in boreal/mixed forest reaches only 0.26-0.61 m point-to-point error (Casseau et al., IROS 2024, ALS vs MLS). Canopy-shape registration of UAV-LiDAR to TLS gives about 0.24 m RMSE (Forests 2025). Photogrammetry sees no stems, so expect the same or worse. Joint SfM of aerial and ground images is a hard open problem. Baseline DUSt3R localizes fewer than 5% of aerial-ground pairs within 5 degrees; fine-tuning on AerialMegaDepth lifts this to about 50-56% (CVPR 2025). That was on buildings; repetitive forest foliage would be worse. It needs bridging views: low oblique orbits at a clearing edge, or the 360 camera on a tall pole.

GNSS FACTS:
- Consumer drones without RTK: about 2.5 m horizontal and 4.5 m vertical RMSE with GPS only (Mini 4 Pro benchmark).
- DJI 'AbsoluteAltitude' / EXIF GPSAltitude on non-RTK drones is barometric and can be 5-50 m off. RelativeAltitude is about ±3 m.
- RTK drones such as the Matrice 4E (built-in RTK, C2, about €4,599) and the Mavic 3E with RTK module are specified at 1 cm + 1 ppm horizontal and 1.5 cm + 1 ppm vertical. RTK drones write ellipsoidal heights, not EGM96, unless the Pilot 2 ASL option is used.
- XMP RtkFlag: 50 = fixed, 34 = float, 16 = single point, 0 = none. Use only images with RtkFlag 50.
- In practice RTK-only blocks are about 2-4 cm horizontal but carry vertical bias. A Phantom 4 RTK study found up to 0.14 m vertical shift, removed by a single GCP, and vertical RMSD of 6-10 cm without GCPs.
- Sweden: SWEPOS network RTK costs 12,000 SEK/yr, 5,000 SEK per 90 days, 2,000 SEK per 30 days, or 5,000 SEK for 1,000 minutes. There is a free 10-day trial, and daily RINEX files are free for PPK. HxGN SmartNet also covers Sweden.
- Under forest canopy, even multi-GNSS RTK degrades to about 0.5-2 m. GCPs must be surveyed in the open or tied in through the LiDAR.

ALIGNMENT CHAIN (adds to the planned ground alignment):
- Existing edge: T_groundSfM->LiDAR, a Sim(3) from trajectory, hand-eye and ICP.
- New edge: T_droneSfM->LiDAR. Make it SE(3) with a scale check if the drone is RTK/PPK, otherwise a full Sim(3). Estimate it from shared targets, ideally inside a GCP-constrained BA (OpenSfM/ODM, Metashape, Leica Infinity). COLMAP has no GCP term in BA, so with COLMAP use pose priors plus a post-hoc Umeyama fit on triangulated target centres.
- Optional edge: T_LiDAR->World, a rigid fit with scale held at 1. Get it either from RTK-rover-surveyed target centres or from target coordinates taken out of the RTK drone model.
- Weakest links, in order: (1) features or targets that both sensors can see (they must be in openings); (2) BLK2GO SLAM drift between the plot and the clearing targets (spec 6-15 mm relative, unproven in forest); (3) drone vertical bias and datum/height-reference confusion (SWEREF 99/ETRS89 vs WGS84/ITRF is about 0.9 m in 2026; ellipsoid vs EGM96 vs RH 2000 heights differ by tens of metres); (4) doming in nadir-only blocks (add 20-35 degree obliques); (5) canopy moving in wind, and leaf or season changes between captures.
- Expected accuracy per step:
  - Target centre in BLK2GO: about 1-2 cm (low confidence).
  - Target in drone images: 0.5-1 px, about 1 cm at 1-1.5 cm GSD.
  - Drone BA with GCPs: about 2 cm horizontal, 3-4 cm vertical.
  - Drone-to-LiDAR at checkpoints: about 3-5 cm.
  - Marker-free: 0.2-0.6 m.
  - GPS only: metres. A non-RTK drone cannot place the LiDAR at all without targets.

GEOREFERENCING (optional): Keep the LiDAR local frame as the working frame W. Store one optional T_W->ECEF (EPSG:4978) together with its CRS and epoch metadata (SWEREF 99 TM EPSG:3006 + RH 2000 EPSG:5613 via the SWEN17_RH2000 geoid, 8-10 mm uncertainty). That 4x4 matrix becomes the 3D Tiles root.transform for Cesium (CesiumJS 1.139+ streams KHR_gaussian_splatting) and the georeference for LuciadRIA. Do the math in float64 and keep local coordinates small. With no LiDAR, W = local ENU from the drone RTK, or an approximate ENU from GPS priors flagged 'low-accuracy'. With splat only, W is the arbitrary SfM frame flagged 'unscaled' unless the user gives a scale bar. The same targets also let the ground splat register to the drone frame when LiDAR is absent.

PRACTICAL: Put 4-6 targets in the first LiDAR + Insta360 test anyway. They cost little, give independent checkpoints for the ground splat-to-LiDAR alignment, and prepare for the drone phase.

## Recommendations

- **Frame design: make the BLK2GO/Cyclone local frame the working frame W whenever LiDAR is present. Model all sources as nodes in a frame graph whose edges carry a Sim(3) 4x4 matrix with scale, a covariance or RMSE, the method used and a validation report. Store one optional T_W->ECEF (EPSG:4978) with CRS and epoch metadata (e.g. SWEREF 99 TM EPSG:3006 + RH 2000 EPSG:5613). Fallbacks: no LiDAR -> W = local ENU at the drone-RTK origin; drone without RTK -> ENU from GPS priors flagged low-accuracy; splat only -> SfM frame flagged 'unscaled' unless the user enters a known distance.** — The BLK2GO has no GNSS, so the LiDAR frame is the only metric frame common to the main use case. World placement is a separate, optional edge, so the viewer works without it and Luciad/Cesium can use it when present. Keeping provenance on each edge lets the UI show alignment quality per source. _Alternatives:_ Use SWEREF 99 TM or ECEF as the working frame. Rejected as the default: large coordinates cause float32 jitter, and it would require GNSS for every project.
- **Primary drone-to-LiDAR registration: shared physical targets. Use 5-8 rigid 40-60 cm B/W checker boards, slightly raised, in a clearing, track or forest edge next to the plot, spread in plan. The BLK2GO walks past each one in the same loop. Measure the target centres in the LiDAR cloud with a plane fit plus intensity or sphere centre, and use these W-frame coordinates as GCPs in a GCP-constrained drone BA (ODM/OpenSfM, Metashape or Leica Infinity). With COLMAP, use pose priors, then triangulate the targets, then a robust Umeyama Sim(3). Keep at least 2 targets as checkpoints.** — This is the only method with cm-level expected accuracy (about 2-5 cm) that does not depend on canopy geometry overlap or on GNSS under canopy. It needs no world survey at all. _Alternatives:_ Marker-free stem/CHM matching (0.2-0.6 m, fails in dense canopy). Drone GPS only (metres, and cannot place the LiDAR). Joint aerial-ground SfM (low success rate).
- **If the drone phase goes ahead, choose an RTK/PPK-capable drone (e.g. DJI Matrice 4E with built-in RTK, C2, about €4.6k, or a Mavic 3E with RTK module). Fly with SWEPOS network RTK (30-day at 2,000 SEK, or the 1,000-minute pot at 5,000 SEK), or do PPK with the free SWEPOS RINEX. Use only images with RtkFlag=50 and include 20-35 degree obliques. Use a consumer drone only with at least 5 targets and a full Sim(3) fit.** — RTK gives metric scale, about 2-4 cm horizontal placement, robustness when only a few targets are visible, and optional georeferencing. Obliques reduce doming. Vertical bias of 5-15 cm still requires at least 1-2 targets or checkpoints. _Alternatives:_ Consumer drone (Mini or Air class) with GPS priors and full GCP control: cheaper, but scale and absolute accuracy depend entirely on the targets.
- **Make georeferencing optional, in one of two ways. (a) Survey the target centres with an RTK rover (e.g. a Leica GS18 through Hexagon, on SWEPOS or SmartNet) in open sky, then fit T_LiDAR->world as a rigid transform with scale fixed at 1, checking the free-scale residual. (b) Without a rover, take the target coordinates from the RTK drone model. Convert with pyproj (SWEN17_RH2000 grid) and record whether coordinates are ETRS89 or ITRF.** — This gives the ECEF placement Luciad and Cesium need at 2-5 cm (rover) or about 5-15 cm vertical (drone RTK only). It avoids the 0.8-0.9 m ETRS89/WGS84 error and height-reference errors of tens of metres. _Alternatives:_ Skip georeferencing and show the model in a local Cartesian reference. This is fine for the first tests.
- **Use ICP and other geometric methods only as a gated refinement after the target-based solution. Run Open3D point-to-plane ICP restricted to cropped overlap (clearing ground, edge stems) with a small max correspondence distance (0.1-0.2 m). Accept the correction only if it is smaller than the target-based uncertainty and checkpoint RMSE does not get worse.** — Canopy geometry seen from above and from below does not correspond. Unconstrained ICP converges to biased solutions under canopy. _Alternatives:_ FPFH+RANSAC global registration, or tree-position graph matching, as a coarse fallback when there are no targets (expect 0.2-0.6 m).
- **Treat joint aerial-ground SfM, with drone and Insta360 frames in one model, as an experimental extra rather than the registration backbone. If tried, add bridging captures (low 5-20 m oblique orbits at the clearing edge, or the 360 camera on a 5-8 m pole) and learned matchers. Use the result only as a consistency check against the target-based drone->LiDAR transform.** — State-of-the-art aerial-ground pose estimation succeeds on only about 50% of pairs after special fine-tuning, in urban scenes. Forests are harder. _Alternatives:_ Register the drone and ground models separately to W through the targets. This is recommended.
- **Put 4-6 of the same targets in the FIRST LiDAR + Insta360 test, without a drone.** — They give independent checkpoints for the planned ground Sim(3)/ICP alignment, test target detection in the BLK2GO cloud and in 360 frames, and rehearse the drone-phase protocol at almost no cost. The drone itself still stays a later phase. _Alternatives:_ Validate the ground alignment only through ICP fitness and visual overlay. Weaker, because self-consistency misses coherent errors.
- **Validation report for every registration edge: checkpoint RMSE (H, V, 3D) on held-out targets; residuals of the targets used; scale ratio |s-1| (expect less than 0.001 with RTK); M3C2/C2C distances on the true overlap (CloudCompare); stem-centre residuals at edge trees; loop consistency drone->W vs drone->groundSfM->W where available. Show a quality badge per layer in the viewer.** — Hold-out and internal consistency cannot certify accuracy. Independent checkpoints and overlap distances are the evidence that counts. _Alternatives:_ None adequate.

## risks

- No co-visible features or targets: if the plot has no clearing, track or edge, the drone cannot be tied to the BLK2GO better than 0.2-0.6 m (marker-free), or at all with photogrammetry under closed tropical canopy.
- BLK2GO SLAM drift between the clearing (where the targets are) and the plot interior. The drone model is accurate at the targets, but the error grows inside the plot. Outdoor forest drift is unspecified.
- Datum and height-reference mix-ups: SWEREF 99/ETRS89 labelled as 'WGS84' (about 0.9 m offset in 2026); DJI barometric AbsoluteAltitude (5-50 m errors); ellipsoidal vs EGM96 vs RH 2000 heights (tens of metres); the Pilot 2 ASL option changing what is written to EXIF.
- Drone RTK vertical bias of 5-15 cm when there are no GCPs or checkpoints. Doming in nadir-only blocks.
- RTK rover cannot fix under canopy (0.5-2 m errors), so world-surveyed GCPs are possible only in the open.
- Joint aerial-ground SfM can fail silently or produce wrong poses (low success rate, repetitive foliage). It must not be the source of truth.
- Unconstrained ICP of the drone canopy surface against the BLK2GO under-canopy returns converges to biased solutions.
- Canopy moving in wind and foliage or lighting changes between drone and ground captures add noise to canopy-based registration and to the splat.
- Target detection in BLK2GO data: intensity distortion at B/W edges, sparse points at range, ground clutter. Needs testing.
- Cyclone Applied Control may not support BLK2GO kinematic data. A custom Python/CloudCompare path is needed.
- License risks: ODM is AGPL; learned aerial-ground matchers (DUSt3R/MASt3R family) are likely non-commercial.
- Float32 precision jitter if world (ECEF/SWEREF) coordinates are used directly in the viewer instead of a local frame plus a 4x4 transform.

## FACT-CHECK: missed developments

- Sweden requires a dissemination permit (spridningstillstånd) from Lantmäteriet to share, publish or sell drone imagery. The report does not mention it, and it directly affects putting drone-derived splats or clouds into a web viewer.: Lantmäteriet: 'Om du har fotograferat eller filmat från luftfartyg som till exempel drönare ... och vill dela, publicera eller sälja dina bilder behöver du ansöka om spridningstillstånd'. Spreading without a permit 'kan vara straffbart'. The requirement covers 'flygfoton, videor, laserdata, höjddata med mera', so derived 3D data is likely covered. Applying is free, but processing currently takes about 65 working days. Forest land is exempt only if the landowner ordered the material or was the photographer, and the images 'får inte innehålla fordon eller andra mänskliga aktiviteter förutom sådant som tillhör skogsbruket'. All exemptions fall away if the image shows surrounding areas sensitive for national defence. Consequences for the plan: (1) get landowner commissioning, or apply about 3 months before publishing; (2) the operator with the BLK2GO, vehicles and possibly the targets may count as human activity; (3) keep drone data private or local until cleared. Ground-only BLK2GO/Insta360 data is not aerial and is not covered. (https://www.lantmateriet.se/spridningstillstand https://www.lantmateriet.se/sv/spridningstillstand/)
- In the EU a C2 drone such as the Matrice 4E can fly in subcategory A3 with only the A1/A3 online training, which suits remote forest test flights. A2 rules (A2 CofC, 30 m or 5 m from people) apply only when flying near people.: Regulation (EU) 2019/947 UAS.OPEN.040(4) allows class C2 (with active remote ID and geo-awareness), C3 and C4 drones, and privately built drones under 25 kg, in A3. A3 requires at least 150 m horizontal distance from residential, commercial, industrial or recreational areas. Note that marked recreational areas or trails in a forest could count. The EASA page confirms A2 for C2: a remote pilot certificate of competency, 30 m from uninvolved people (5 m in low-speed mode), and a 120 m AGL limit, with the rules applying since 1 Jan 2024. No 2024-2026 amendment was found, but the search budget was exhausted. (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02019R0947-20220404 https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones-rpas/open-category-civil-drones)

It is not a usable drone-to-BLK2GO registration path under canopy.: opendm/align.py calls codem.coarse_registration (DSM), then codem.fine_registration (ICP), reads get_point_cloud_crs and get_raster_crs from both inputs, and sets DSM_SOLVE_SCALE=False and ICP_SOLVE_SCALE=False. A Cyclone local-frame LAS has no CRS, and a BLK2GO 'DSM' under canopy is understory or lower crown, not the canopy top that drone SfM sees. At most it could work in clearings, after the LAS has been given a fake projected CRS. ## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The core recommendations hold up: the LiDAR local frame as W, plus an optional T_W->ECEF with CRS and epoch, and shared physical targets in a clearing, edge or track as the primary drone-to-LiDAR tie. So do the three other placements: RTK/PPK as an aid rather than a substitute, marker-free ICP only as gated refinement, and joint aerial-ground SfM as experimental. None of the load-bearing evidence was refuted. Casseau (0.26-0.61 m, ALS-MLS), Forests 2025 (0.24 m), AerialMegaDepth (<5% -> ~56%), Štroner 2020 (0.06-0.10 m RTK-only vertical, a 0.14 m shift fixed by 1 GCP), the DJI RtkFlag codes and ellipsoidal heights, SWEPOS prices and the SWEREF 99/WGS84 offset of 0.8-0.9 m (Lantmäteriet primary source) were all confirmed. Several marker-free numbers come from LiDAR-to-LiDAR work, so they are optimistic bounds for photogrammetry, as the report says.

Changes I would make:
(1) Add the Swedish spridningstillstånd as a hard gate for publishing any drone-derived layer. It is free but takes about 65 working days. Forest is exempt only if the landowner commissioned the material and no non-forestry human activity or vehicles are visible. Until it is cleared, drone layers stay local or private. This is a schedule risk for option (b) or (c) and does not affect the first LiDAR + Insta360 test.
(2) Drop ODM --align from the toolbox for this use. It needs CRS-tagged inputs, matches DSMs with scale fixed at 1, and fails conceptually under canopy.
(3) GCP-in-local-frame is still unproven for ODM/OpenSfM. Plan to run SfM with drone priors and then a custom robust SE(3)/Sim(3) fit on triangulated target centres (pycolmap/pyceres), or use Metashape/Infinity local coordinates. Test ODM with a fake projected CRS only as an option.
(4) Target design: size boards at least 20x GSD on the diagonal if auto-detection is wanted (the Pix4D AutoGCP attribution in the report was swapped). Add retro-reflective or geometric features for the BLK2GO, because white paint does not contrast with vegetation in LiDAR intensity. Validate detection in the first ground test, which the report already recommends.
(5) Update the stale facts. KHR_gaussian_splatting is ratified; CesiumJS has supported it since 1.135 and is now at 1.146. COLMAP EXIF gravity priors came in 4.1, not 4.2. Metashape reads XMP AbsoluteAltitude, not EXIF GPSAltitude. BLK2GO absolute accuracy on the cited page is ±10 mm indoor, and the 6-15 mm/20 mm figures are unverified. The Mini 4 Pro numbers are a compilation, so treat them as indicative.
(6) Regulation: a C2 Matrice 4E may fly in A3 with only A1/A3 training at least 150 m from residential or recreational areas. Remote forest test flights therefore do not need the A2 CofC.
(7) Leica Infinity PPK support for the M4E and HxGN SmartNet coverage of Sweden are unverified; ask Hexagon internally.

The drone-to-LiDAR accuracy estimate of 2-5 cm near the targets is plausible but rests on an unverified BLK2GO target-centre accuracy and unknown forest SLAM drift, so keep it at medium-to-low confidence until the first test measures checkpoint residuals.

Note: the shared web-search budget ran out early in this check, so checking relied on direct fetches of primary URLs. The doming/oblique figures (Wiley/MDPI 403), the Forests 2025 manual-registration figure, the DJI Pilot 2 ASL option and Leica's own BLK2GO datasheet could not be checked.
