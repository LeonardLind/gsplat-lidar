# insta360

## Summary

STATE OF THE ART (2026-10-06). Insta360's current X-series 360 cameras are the X6 (launched 12 Aug 2026, $699.99), X5 (22 Apr 2025), X4 Air (28 Oct 2025, 165 g, 1/1.8") and X4 (16 Apr 2024, 1/2"). The ONE RS 1-Inch 360 (2022, 6K) is discontinued, and there is no current Insta360 "pro" 8K rig worth considering here. Two models matter for this project. The X6 has dual square 1/1.1" Sony sensors (101 mm², 33% more area than the X5), F2.0, and records 8K 7680x3840 at up to 50 fps and 6K at 60 fps. It encodes H.265 in 10-bit (Standard, Dolby Vision or I-Log), has a 16K 15360x7680 timelapse, takes 120 MP INSP/DNG stills, weighs 196 g and runs about 140 min at 8K30. The X5 has 1/1.28" sensors and records 8K30 / 5.7K60 at 180 Mbps in H.264 or H.265. Its I-Log is limited to 5.7K60 and below and appears to be 8-bit (third-party claim; Insta360 gives no bit depth for X5 I-Log). It takes 72 MP INSP/DNG stills, has an Interval photo mode, weighs 203 g and runs about 93 min at 8K30. On both, ISO is 100-6400 and video shutter runs from 1/8000 s up to 1/fps, so manual exposure is possible. According to the X6 manual, normal video tops out at 180 Mbps, with 240 Mbps only for 8K timelapse/TimeShift and 360 Mbps for 16K timelapse. Some reviews say 240 Mbps for video. At 8K50 the X6 therefore gets fewer bits per frame than at 8K30.

FILE FORMAT. At 5.7K and above, X4, X4 Air, X5 and X6 write ONE .insv per clip. It is an MP4 container holding two square HEVC fisheye tracks plus AAC audio (48 kHz, 16-bit) and a binary trailer at the end of the file. Track sizes are 2880² or 3840², 8-bit full-range BT.709 on X5; 3840² HEVC Main10, BT.2020 HLG, limited range on X6. X3 and older wrote one file per lens (_00_/_10_). The trailer records are:
- an info protobuf with rolling-shutter time (ms), a measured gyro offset (1.6 ms on X5) and factory lens calibration. The calibration is a Mei/unified model with xi, fx, fy, cx, cy, yaw, pitch, roll, tx, ty, tz and distortion. X5 uses v3; X6 has only v6, which adds k1..k5.
- gyro+accel. On X5 these are raw 20-byte samples with a microsecond timestamp, at 1 kHz.
- an exposure record per captured frame (timestamp of the frame's middle row + exposure time).
- per-frame timestamps and GPS.

The IMU, the exposure entries and the first-frame timestamp all share one clock, so per-frame exposure-centre times and 1 kHz angular rates can be recovered offline. One open-source stitcher (insv-stitch) uses a ~21 ms X5 rolling-shutter readout. FFmpeg can stream-copy the two lens tracks losslessly; it only uses track 0 by default. LRV files are low-res proxies.

TOOLING. Insta360's Desktop MediaSDK and CameraSDK (C++ only, Windows x64 / Ubuntu 22.04, obtained by application, no public licence) list X2-X5 but not X6. MediaSDK offers TEMPLATE, OPTFLOW, DYNAMICSTITCH and AIFLOW stitching, FlowState and Direction Lock toggles, chromatic calibration, denoise and frame-sequence export, with equirect MP4/JPG output only. Open-source support:
- telemetry-parser (MIT/Apache, Python wheels for Windows): last PyPI release v0.3.0 (16 Jan 2026). X5 support was merged on 4 Feb 2026, after that release, so it needs a build from master. No X6 support.
- GyroView (MIT): the most complete public spec of the format. It parses X3, X4, X5 and X6 (X6 support merged 1 Oct 2026).
- Gyroflow v1.6.3: does not support stabilising 360 cameras.
- ExifTool: decodes the generic Insta360 trailer (gyro/accel, exposure, timestamps, GPS).

STITCHED vs RAW FISHEYE. A stitched equirect is not a single-centre projection. It carries two-lens parallax at the seams: an open-source X5 stitcher reports about 18 px of ghosting for objects closer than 3 m, which is exactly the forest-trunk range. Optical-flow stitching changes that warp from frame to frame, and FlowState and Direction Lock add per-frame rotation and rolling-shutter warps. Each fisheye lens, by contrast, is a true central camera with factory intrinsics and a factory lens-to-lens transform. For metric registration to LiDAR, and above all for estimating the BLK2GO↔Insta360 extrinsic, the camera's orientation must stay fixed to the body. That means no FlowState and no Direction Lock (or using raw fisheye directly), because stabilised output breaks the constant extrinsic.

COLMAP now covers both routes:
- 4.0.0 (Mar 2026) integrated GLOMAP as a global mapper.
- 4.1.0 (Jun 2026) added spherical/equirect and EUCM camera models.
- 4.2.1 (29 Sep 2026) is the latest release.
- Rigs are supported, and panorama_sfm.py renders virtual pinhole views from equirect frames as a rig.

On the training side, gsplat (Apache-2.0) supports fisheye and 3DGUT, the CVPR 2025 method that handles distorted cameras and rolling shutter. LichtFeld Studio (GPLv3) also has 3DGUT. A CMU MSCV 2025 study with an X4 found rig-based SfM on equirect walks clearly beat Stella-VSLAM: PSNR 22.48 vs 19.79, LPIPS 0.395 vs 0.484.

PRACTICE. Community and vendor guides (Prevu3d, FreeGaussian, RealHorizons, Splatware, the LichtFeld 360 plugin, 360 Gaussian Pro) agree on the following:
- shoot the highest native resolution with high bitrate;
- lock exposure;
- avoid heavy stabilisation;
- walk slowly at a constant height on a rigid stick (no gimbal);
- clean the lenses and calibrate the IMU before capture;
- mask the operator (SAM 3 in the plugins);
- split into 6-24 virtual pinhole views at 90° FOV with a rig constraint, or use fisheye directly.

Prevu3d goes further and recommends Timelapse 360 mode at a 0.5 s interval. That is a real option on the X6, where 16K timelapse records full-resolution frames, but timelapse loses the audio track the user needs for the clap (not verified). Insta360's own "Spatial Capture" (cloud, about 4 generations per month) and the Splatica partnership are cloud services and do not fit the all-local requirement.

IMPLICATIONS FOR THIS PROJECT:
1. Capture in normal 360 Video mode at 8K30 or 8K25: the most bits per frame, and it keeps audio and the 1 kHz IMU. On X6, use 8K50 only if you want denser frame selection.
2. Set manual shutter to 1/500-1/1000 s, fixed ISO (auto-ISO capped) and fixed white balance. Sharpness low/medium. On X5 use Standard/Flat, not 8-bit I-Log; on X6, 10-bit I-Log or Standard, tone-mapped to SDR consistently. Turn off AdaptiveTone, Active HDR and PureVideo.
3. Pull the raw .insv, demux both fisheye tracks with FFmpeg, and parse the trailer (IMU, per-frame exposure timestamps, factory calibration) in Python.
4. Run SfM either as a 2-lens fisheye rig, or on undistorted pinhole crops arranged as a rig with fixed relative poses. Directly reprojecting the fisheye or equirect frames (OpenCV remap or FFmpeg v360) is better than rendering virtual cameras in Blender.
5. Sync the BLK2GO and the Insta360 by correlating the Insta360's 1 kHz gyro with the angular rate of the BLK2GO trajectory; do a deliberate twist/tap at the start and end. A clap only marks the Insta360's own timeline, because the BLK2GO records no audio.
6. Model choice: if you are buying, get the X6 for sensor size, 10-bit and lower power draw (good for tropical heat), and budget for writing your own X6 trailer parser using GyroView's FORMAT.md. If an X5 is already available, it is the lowest-risk camera for the Swedish test.

Note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Insta360 Studio's current export menu (ProRes on Windows, any dual-fisheye export), the X6 IMU rate and readout time, and how timelapse mode handles audio and IMU were not verified from primary sources.

## Recommendations

- **Camera choice: if buying now, use the Insta360 X6 (1/1.1" sensors, 10-bit, 8K30/50, 16K timelapse, 2600 mAh/140 min, 35% lower power claimed, which helps in tropical heat). If an X5 is already at hand, use it for the Swedish pilot: it has the most mature open tooling (telemetry-parser X5 support Feb 2026, insv-stitch, GyroView primarily verified on X5, Insta360 MediaSDK lists X5).** — Under forest canopy, low light and sensor size dominate image quality; the X6 has 33% more sensor area than the X5 and 10-bit. The X6 trade-off is tooling: it is not in the Insta360 SDK README or telemetry-parser, it uses a new v6 calibration string, a rotated IMU frame and HLG/BT.2020 video. A planner must budget a custom Python trailer parser (following GyroView FORMAT.md, MIT) and HLG->SDR tone mapping. _Alternatives:_ X4 Air (165 g, lighter for pole use, but 1/1.8" sensors); X4 (1/2", weakest). DJI Osmo 360 / Osmo 360 II (1-inch, 8K50/60) are viable non-Insta360 alternatives; the LichtFeld 360 plugin reads .osv.
- **Record in normal 360 Video mode (not Timelapse/TimeShift/PureVideo/Active HDR) at 8K and 24/25/30 fps, H.265, High bitrate, raw .insv kept off the SD card. On X6 consider 8K50 only if denser frame selection is needed.** — Video mode keeps the audio clap and the continuous 1 kHz IMU plus per-frame exposure timestamps. 8K30 gives more bits per frame than 8K50 at the same 180 Mbps cap. PureVideo/AI denoise and Active HDR introduce temporally varying processing that harms multi-view consistency. _Alternatives:_ For a separate Run-2-style high-detail pass on X6: 16K Timelapse 360 at about 0.5 s interval (Prevu3d practice), accepting no audio and possibly longer rolling-shutter readout. Or Interval DNG stills while standing still at stations (highest detail, slow).
- **Exposure: manual shutter 1/500 s (1/1000 s in good light), fixed ISO (start at 400-800 under canopy; the X5 should stay at or below ~800 if possible), fixed Kelvin white balance, sharpness Low or Medium, colour Standard or Flat on X5 (avoid its 8-bit I-Log, which is unavailable at 8K anyway), Standard or 10-bit I-Log on X6 with one fixed conversion LUT/tone-map for all frames. Disable AdaptiveTone, HDR and auto-ISO jumps. Calibrate the gyro on a level surface before each session and clean the lenses.** — At ~1 m/s walking and ~20 px/deg, 1/500 s keeps translational blur near 1-2 px at 1-2 m range (my calculation). Locked exposure and WB avoid per-frame appearance changes that degrade feature matching and splat colour. Consumer guides that recommend auto exposure/WB are optimising for video viewing, not reconstruction. _Alternatives:_ Shutter priority with an ISO cap if light varies strongly along the loop; then rely on per-image appearance embeddings in the trainer.
- **Primary processing path: demux the two fisheye HEVC tracks losslessly from the .insv (ffmpeg -map 0:v:0 / 0:v:1 -c copy). Parse the trailer in Python (IMU, exposure timestamps, offset_v3/v6 calibration, rolling-shutter ms, gyro offset). Run SfM in COLMAP 4.2.x as a 2-sensor rig. Either (a) fisheye cameras (EUCM/OPENCV_FISHEYE, initialised from the factory calibration with the lens-to-lens extrinsic as rig prior), or (b) 4-6 undistorted pinhole crops per lens (~90-100° FOV) declared as a rig with fixed relative rotations. Train with a fisheye/3DGUT-capable open trainer (gsplat or LichtFeld) or on the pinhole crops.** — This avoids stitching parallax (~18 px ghosting under 3 m), frame-varying optical-flow warps and stabilisation re-rotation. It keeps a central projection per lens and the body-fixed orientation that the BLK2GO↔Insta360 extrinsic estimate requires. Factory calibration gives good priors. _Alternatives:_ Equirect path: export from Insta360 Studio with FlowState OFF and Direction Lock OFF (optical-flow stitching, max bitrate), then either COLMAP 4.1+ spherical camera model or virtual pinhole rig (COLMAP panorama_sfm.py / LichtFeld 360 plugin, 6-24 views at 90°). Run both on the first dataset and compare reprojection error and LiDAR cloud-to-cloud error.
- **Replace the Blender virtual-camera idea with direct reprojection in Python: OpenCV remap or py360convert from fisheye or equirect to pinhole, or FFmpeg's v360 filter (dfisheye/fisheye/equirect input to flat output, yaw/pitch/roll, h_fov/v_fov).** — Direct remapping is exact, scriptable, GPU/NVDEC-friendly and reproducible. Blender adds a render step with its own sampling and no geometric benefit; the virtual camera poses still have to be written into the COLMAP rig config by hand. The v360 options come from background knowledge; the docs page could not be read in full. _Alternatives:_ Blender 'Camarray' camera-array tool (paid gumroad) if a visual setup is preferred.
- **Time sync: use IMU cross-correlation as the primary method and the clap as a coarse check. Correlate the Insta360 1 kHz gyro (µs timestamps, same clock as per-frame mid-row exposure stamps) with angular velocity from the time-stamped BLK2GO SLAM trajectory. Make 3 sharp yaw twists plus a knock on the rig at the start and end of each run, and fit offset + linear drift.** — The BLK2GO records no audio, so the clap cannot link the two devices on its own. The rotational signature is sensed by both devices and gives sub-frame accuracy. Start and end events reveal clock drift. _Alternatives:_ Clap/knock visible in the BLK2GO's own camera imagery, if its images carry timestamps (to be checked by the LiDAR research thread).
- **Mounting: replace tape with a rigid bracket. Orient the Insta360 lenses to face left/right of the walking direction (seams forward/back along the path) and put the BLK2GO and the operator's hand in the seam/nadir blind zone as far as possible. Mask the BLK2GO, hand and operator with SAM 3 per fisheye lens. For Run 2, use a rigid carbon pole 0.5-1 m above head height.** — A rigid mount keeps the extrinsic constant (tape will creep and vibrate). The lateral views carry the most parallax for a loop path. Close objects in the upper hemisphere (the BLK2GO body) occupy a large solid angle and would corrupt SfM and splats unless masked. Also check that the camera does not occlude the BLK2GO's LiDAR field of view. _Alternatives:_ Mount the camera above the BLK2GO on an extension, if the BLK2GO handle allows it.
- **Read the rolling-shutter time (info protobuf field 25) and the gyro offset per file. Either apply gyro-based per-scanline rolling-shutter correction before SfM (as insv-stitch does: 32 SLERP keyframes over ~21 ms on X5), or use a rolling-shutter-aware trainer (3DGUT). Walk slowly and avoid fast yaw.** — At ~21 ms readout and normal handheld yaw rates, rolling-shutter skew (~1° or about 25 px) exceeds motion blur by an order of magnitude and biases the poses used for LiDAR registration. _Alternatives:_ Accept the rolling-shutter error for the pilot and quantify it with LiDAR cloud-to-cloud residuals.

## risks

- X6 tooling gap: not listed in Insta360 Media/Camera SDK READMEs (updated Jul 2026) or in telemetry-parser (no X6 commits). The X6 uses a new v6 calibration string, an IMU frame rotated a quarter turn vs X5, and 10-bit HLG BT.2020 video, so a custom parser and tone mapping are required. GyroView's X6 notes are provisional, from one recording.
- telemetry-parser's X5 support (merged 4 Feb 2026) is newer than the latest PyPI release v0.3.0 (16 Jan 2026), so pip install may not handle X5 correctly; build from master or write your own parser.
- The trailer format is reverse-engineered and has changed with firmware (contiguous vs indexed records, float vs raw IMU layouts). New firmware can break parsers; record the firmware version (X6 firmware 1.1.7 as of 24 Aug 2026 per a third-party tracker).
- Bitrate ambiguity for X6 video (manual: 180 Mbps for normal modes; reviews: 240 Mbps). 8K50 gives about 40% fewer bits per frame than 8K30, so compression artefacts may limit fine forest detail (leaves, needles).
- Rolling shutter (~21 ms on X5; X6 unknown) during handheld walking causes geometric errors larger than motion blur. Ignoring it will bias SfM poses and the Insta360↔LiDAR extrinsic estimate.
- Stitched-equirect workflows (Studio export, MediaSDK) put seam parallax (~18 px for objects under 3 m) and frame-varying optical-flow warps into data near trunks. FlowState/Direction Lock make the extrinsic time-varying, which invalidates offset estimation.
- Taping the camera under the BLK2GO: unstable extrinsic, the BLK2GO body and hand are large occluders in the 360 view, and the camera may occlude the LiDAR's field of view.
- Forest-specific: wind-moving foliage and sky/canopy are non-static, so they need masking or robust losses. Low light under canopy forces high ISO (noise). In tropical heat and humidity, the 8K power draw risks overheating (X5/X6 rated only to 40 °C) and lens fogging.
- Clap sync: timelapse/interval modes likely have no audio, and the BLK2GO has no audio, so the clap alone cannot sync the devices.
- Insta360 Studio's current export options (dual-fisheye export, ProRes on Windows, AI stitching availability) were not verified: insta360.com returned 403 and the shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-research.
- The proprietary SDK requires an application and has no public licence; avoid making it a pipeline dependency.

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The main recommendations hold, but six of them need changes. The shared WebSearch budget was exhausted before this check started, so everything here comes from about 75 direct fetches of primary pages (manuals, GitHub, PyPI, changelogs).

What holds:
- Capture in normal 360 Video mode at 8K 24/25/30 and keep the raw .insv. This is now stronger: battery is about 79 min at 8K50 against about 140 min at 8K30, the bitrate is the same, and Prevu3d's timelapse guidance only adds an option on top.
- Use manual shutter, fixed ISO and fixed WB.
- Demux both fisheye tracks with FFmpeg and parse the trailer yourself.
- Avoid Studio/MediaSDK stitching and stabilisation for SfM and extrinsic work. The X6 manual now confirms that the raw 360 .insv is never stabilised in-camera.
- Sync by cross-correlating gyro data, with the clap only as a coarse check.
- Use a rigid mount and SAM 3 masking.
- Choose the X6 if buying, or the X5 if one is already at hand.
- COLMAP 4.2.x with a rig, or with the native EQUIRECTANGULAR model added in 4.1.0, is confirmed.

Changes:
1. **telemetry-parser.** The PyPI release v0.3.0 is from 16 Jan 2024, not 2026. Its Windows wheels stop at cp312 and it has no X5 or X6 support. Do not depend on pip. Write a small Python trailer parser following GyroView's FORMAT.md and telemetry-parser's record.rs and mod.rs. Use raw u16 scaled by range/32768. Store all 27 v6 calibration tokens, not just k1-k5, p1 and p2. Do not use ExifTool for IMU units: its fixed /1000 scale is probably wrong for the raw layout.
2. **Reprojection.** FFmpeg v360 is not 'exact', because it ignores xi, distortion and principal point. Use OpenCV-contrib omnidir (Mei) or a custom NumPy remap built on the factory offset_v3/v6 calibration.
3. **Training.** gsplat's stock COLMAP loader undistorts OPENCV_FISHEYE. It rejects EUCM, equirect and rigs, and its simple_trainer exposes no rolling-shutter option. Native fisheye or rolling-shutter training needs a custom loader (camera_model='fisheye', with_ut, MCMC), and the factory Mei model must be refit to OpenCV/KB form. Also evaluate NVIDIA 3DGRUT (Apache-2.0, Windows support added, rolling shutter, multiple sensors). For the pilot, keep 'pinhole crops rig' as the low-risk path: Brush and most trainers accept pinhole only.
4. **X6 colour.** Normal Video mode is 'HDR by default', Standard is also 10-bit, and colour is signalled only in the SPS. Plan deterministic HLG-to-SDR conversion for all X6 footage, not just I-Log. 'Disable Active HDR' may not be possible on X6, so test whether in-camera HDR is consistent from frame to frame.
5. **Run-2 timelapse.** Prevu3d uses 8K, not 16K, timelapse on X6. Interval minimums depend on resolution (0.5 s at best). Whether timelapse records audio or the IMU is still unverified, so verify on-device before relying on 16K timelapse.
6. **Small spec fixes:**
   - X5 is about 200 g and rated to 15 m; X4 is 200 Mbps.
   - The inter-lens baseline is about 30 mm (insv-stitch), which partly answers the open question on seam parallax.
   - LichtFeld's 360 plugin estimates fisheye intrinsics by bundle adjustment and does not use the factory calibration or the IMU. It is useful but not a replacement for the rig prior.
   - Spatial Capture being 'cloud' is unverified; it is in-app, proprietary and quota-limited, so it is excluded either way.
