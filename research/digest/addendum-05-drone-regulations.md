# drone-regulations

## Summary

REGULATORY PICTURE (as of 2026-10-06). The legal side changes the drone decision. A ground-only capture (option a: BLK2GO + Insta360 carried on foot) is outside Sweden's law on protection of geographical information. Lag 2016:319 §9 bans spreading compilations of geographic information about Swedish land only when "informationen har inhämtats från luftfartyg", and a drone counts as an aircraft. So option (a) can be published on the web without a Lantmäteriet permit; only GDPR applies (blur faces and number plates). Any drone-derived product needs a dissemination permit (spridningstillstånd) before it is put on the web, emailed to others, or stored on cloud servers or other servers you do not fully control. That covers photos, video, orthophotos, laser and height data and 3D models, and a Gaussian splat almost certainly counts as a 3D model. The permit is free. Lantmäteriet's estimate is now about 65 working days (about 3 months). Lantmäteriet cannot retouch 3D models, so a model is approved or refused as a whole. The rule still stands: the only 2026 amendment (SFS 2026:1378, in force 1 Aug 2026) changed §6, which covers exemptions for authorities and photography during heightened readiness, not the §9 permit duty. Riksdag motion 2025/26:2626, which asked for a review, was rejected. Lantmäteriet's exemptions include forest land, but only if the landowner ordered or took the material, it shows no vehicles or human activity other than forestry, it shows no power lines, and no wider surroundings are visible. A drone grid over a test plot will often show roads or buildings, so plan to apply. The architecture consequence is direct. Option (b), a merged splat, puts the whole splat under the permit duty, and the ground layer cannot be published on its own. Option (c), a separate aerial layer, lets the ground model be published right away while the drone layer waits for or fails the permit. The source-module design should therefore carry a per-source 'dissemination clearance' flag, and the public build should leave out uncleared aerial layers.

EU AVIATION FRAMEWORK. Regulations (EU) 2019/947 (operations) and 2019/945 (product classes C0–C6) apply. Open category: VLOS, at most 120 m above the closest point of the terrain, MTOM under 25 kg.
- A1: C0 drones (<250 g) may fly over uninvolved people but never over assemblies; C1 (<900 g) must not overfly uninvolved people.
- A2: C2 (<4 kg), at least 30 m from uninvolved people, or 5 m in low-speed mode; needs the A2 certificate of competency (CofC).
- A3: C2/C3/C4 and legacy drones; no uninvolved person may be endangered, and the drone must stay at least 150 m from residential, commercial, industrial or recreational areas. The regulation does not define a recreational area, so the operator has to judge forest trails and recreation areas.
- Legacy drones (no class label, placed on the market before 1 Jan 2024) may still fly: under 250 g in A1, otherwise only in A3.
- Direct Remote ID has been mandatory since 1 Jan 2024 for C1, C2, C3, C5 and C6, and for the Specific category. C0, C4 and legacy drones are exempt. The operator registration number must be uploaded to the drone.
- Insurance: Regulation 785/2004 exempts model aircraft under 20 kg.
- Specific category: STS-01/02 with C5/C6 drones, PDRA, or SORA 2.5 (ED Decision 2025/018/R). It is needed only for BVLOS, flights above 120 m, flights over assemblies or drones over 25 kg; a small forest grid fits in Open.

Coming change: EASA Opinion 06/2026 (18 Sep 2026, sent to the Commission, not yet adopted) would lower registration, Remote ID and training thresholds from 250 g to 100 g and require identity checks of operators. Drones under 900 g placed on the market before entry into force + 12 months would not need a Remote ID add-on. Press reports expect the first effects around mid-2028. Buy a class-labelled drone with Remote ID.

SWEDEN (Transportstyrelsen):
- Operator registration is required if the drone is 250 g or more, or carries a camera or sensor (so any camera drone). It costs 190 SEK to apply and 250 SEK per year, billed to anyone registered on 1 January. Display the operator ID on the drone.
- A1/A3 card: online test, 40 questions, 75% to pass, 600 SEK, valid 5 years. It is mandatory for drones of 250 g or more. Flying without it has been a crime since 1 Jan 2026 (30–150 day-fines, up to 6 months' prison in the worst cases).
- A2 card: an extra 30-question exam, which per Transportstyrelsen must now be taken under supervision at an authorised drone school (ADS; a secondary source dates this to 23 Oct 2025), plus a declared practical self-training. 850 SEK, or 600 SEK if the A1/A3 fee is already paid.
- Insurance is not required for leisure drones under 20 kg. For work flights, check that Hexagon's liability insurance covers drones.
- Airspace: check the LFV drone map (dronechart.lfv.se) and NOTAM/AIP SUP. Within 5 km of a runway, ATC permission is always needed. Elsewhere in a control zone (CTR), the limit is 50 m AGL, or 10 m at military airports, at speeds of 25 m/s or less. Coordinate with any heliport within 1,000 m, such as hospital helipads. Never fly over or next to a designated protected site (skyddsobjekt) or in R-areas.
- Protected nature: landing is banned in all national parks without a Länsstyrelsen permit, and some parks also have Transportstyrelsen flight restrictions. Nature reserves may ban take-off and landing, so check the reserve's rules with the Länsstyrelsen. Bird protection areas often have a seasonal access ban, typically 1 Apr–15 Jul. Deliberately disturbing breeding birds is banned under artskyddsförordningen 4 §.
- Landowner: allemansrätten does not cover drone operations. Take-off or landing that causes appreciable damage or nuisance needs the landowner's consent. Get written consent anyway, because it also enables the forest exemption.
- Privacy: GDPR and the camera surveillance act apply only if people or number plates can be identified. A drone flown on site for a mapping session is generally not 'camera surveillance', which means ongoing or regularly repeated monitoring of people. Since 1 Aug 2026 (SFS 2026:1373) no one needs a camera surveillance permit.

TROPICS. Rules depend on the country and are often much stricter for foreigners. An EU A1/A3 certificate is generally not recognised.
- Brazil: RBAC 100 has been in force since 16 Jun 2026. Drones over 250 g must be registered in SISANT, which needs a CPF. Every flight needs DECEA SARPAS authorisation, the drone needs ANATEL homologation, the ANAC exam is enforced from 1 Jan 2027, and third-party insurance is mandatory.
- Malaysia: CAAM Authorisation to Fly (ATF), applied for at least 14 working days ahead. JUPEM approval is needed for aerial photo, mapping or LiDAR. Sabah and Sarawak require extra authorisation.
- Costa Rica: DGAC registration and authorisation. SINAC protected areas also need an environmental permit (MINAE).
- Indonesia: foreigners cannot register a drone and must work through a local company; national parks need a BKSDA permit.
Budget months, and use a local licensed operator.

NET RECOMMENDATION FROM A REGULATORY VIEW. Keep phase 1 ground-only. If a drone is added, use a C0 or C1 drone in A1, or a C2 in A3 over the forest. Process the images locally, not in cloud photogrammetry. Show the drone data as a separate layer (option c), and apply for the permit about 3 months before publishing.

## Recommendations

- **Keep phase 1 (Swedish forest test) ground-only, with BLK2GO and Insta360 on foot. Regulation is one more reason not to bring the drone earlier.** — Ground-captured data is outside lag 2016:319, so no aviation paperwork, Lantmäteriet permit or 3-month wait is needed, and the result can go on the web right away (after blurring faces and plates). Adding a drone brings registration, competency, a site and zone check, landowner consent and, before publishing, a permit taking about 65 working days that can be refused with no retouching option. _Alternatives:_ If a drone must be tested early, fly it for internal R&D only. Keep the data on systems you control, and publish only after a permit is granted.
- **If drone imagery is added in Sweden, show it as a separate aerial layer (option c) rather than merging it into the ground splat (option b), at least for anything that will be published.** — A merged splat is drone-derived as a whole and needs a permit before any web sharing, and Lantmäteriet cannot retouch 3D models. A separate layer keeps the ground model publishable and confines permit risk to the aerial layer. _Alternatives:_ Train both a merged splat (internal and evaluation use) and a ground-only splat (public build), and publish the merged one only after approval.
- **Add a per-source 'dissemination_clearance' attribute to the common source-module interface. For example: ground sources = cleared; drone = pending/approved/denied, with the Lantmäteriet case number. The public viewer build should leave out uncleared layers and any model trained on uncleared data.** — This turns the legal rule into a build-time gate, so drone-derived content is not published or uploaded by mistake. Uploading to cloud or third-party servers already counts as dissemination. _Alternatives:_ A manual release checklist only (more error-prone).
- **Process drone imagery locally on the RTX 3080 Ti laptop (COLMAP/GLOMAP and an open-source splat trainer). Do not use cloud photogrammetry or cloud splat services for Swedish drone data until a permit is granted.** — Lantmäteriet counts storage on 'molntjänster eller på servrar som du inte själv har fullständig kontroll över' as dissemination. _Alternatives:_ A Hexagon-controlled on-prem server may count as internal, but confirm with Lantmäteriet or Hexagon legal.
- **For the first drone flights, use a class-labelled drone with Remote ID. C0 (for example, Mini 5 Pro class) or C1 allows A1 with the least friction. Choose C2 (for example, Matrice 4E with mechanical shutter) only if image quality needs it, and fly it in A3 away from people. Get the A1/A3 card (600 SEK) even for a C0 drone.** — C0/C1 in A1 avoids A3's rule of 150 m from recreational areas, which is unclear in public-access forests. Legacy non-class drones over 250 g are limited to A3. EASA Opinion 06/2026 will push training and registration down to 100 g, and flying a drone of 250 g or more without a card has been a crime since 1 Jan 2026. _Alternatives:_ C2 with the A2 certificate (850 SEK, supervised exam at a drone school) to fly within 30 m (5 m in low-speed mode) of uninvolved people.
- **Register the operator as the legal entity that runs the flights (likely the Hexagon unit, through a company signatory) and confirm that drones are covered by its liability insurance and internal drone and data policies.** — These are work flights, not leisure. Transportstyrelsen's insurance exemption is worded for leisure, and the operator is also the GDPR controller. _Alternatives:_ The user registers as a private operator (190 + 250 SEK per year) for a pure personal test, but then the data's ownership and use for Hexagon get complicated.
- **Include in the plan's drone phase a step-by-step pre-flight checklist: (1) choose the drone class and subcategory; (2) register the operator, label the drone with the operator ID and enter it into Remote ID; (3) get the A1/A3 card, plus A2 if a C2 will fly near people; (4) check insurance; (5) check the LFV drone map, NOTAM and AIP SUP for control zones (5 km rule, 50 m or 10 m limits), R-areas, UAS zones, heliports within 1,000 m and protected sites; (6) check the site's protection status (national park, nature reserve rules, bird protection season, breeding birds) and get a Länsstyrelsen permit if needed; (7) get written landowner consent that covers 'ordering' the material; (8) plan the flight within Open limits (VLOS from a clearing, possibly with an observer, at most 120 m AGL, daylight, distances to uninvolved people); (9) avoid capturing identifiable people and plan blurring; (10) keep data local; (11) apply for the permit about 3 months before any publication, marking the area on a map; (12) keep flight logs and certificates.** — This covers every requirement found and the order in which each one blocks the next. _Alternatives:_ Hire a Swedish licensed drone service company that handles operator duties. The dissemination permit is still needed for publication.
- **For tropical campaigns, budget a country-specific legal review months ahead and plan to partner with a locally licensed operator. Check the aviation authority's registration (often closed to foreigners), per-flight airspace approval, radio homologation and customs, mapping or survey and security approvals (for example, Malaysia JUPEM for aerial photo, mapping and LiDAR), protected-area permits, research or fieldwork permits for foreigners, and any limits on exporting or publishing geodata.** — Brazil, Malaysia, Costa Rica and Indonesia all impose extra approvals, and EU certificates are generally not recognised. _Alternatives:_ Run tropical tests ground-only (LiDAR + 360 camera) to avoid aviation permits. Protected-area and research permits may still apply.

## risks

- The dissemination permit for drone-derived models can be refused or delayed (about 65 working days now, with record backlogs reported). Lantmäteriet cannot retouch 3D models, so a merged splat (option b) could become unpublishable as a whole.
- Uploading drone images or models to any cloud or third-party server (cloud photogrammetry, CDN hosting, sharing links) counts as dissemination before a permit. This affects pipeline and hosting choices.
- Relying on the forest exemption is legally fragile. A visible road, vehicle, power line, building or wide view of the surroundings voids it, and the person disseminating carries the risk.
- A3 operations (C2/C3/legacy drones) need 150 m from 'recreational areas', which the regulation does not define, and Swedish forests are open to the public. C0/C1 in A1 avoids this.
- VLOS is hard to keep in a forest. Launching from clearings and staying above the canopy is required in the Open category; flying under the canopy quickly loses VLOS.
- The test site may lie in a control zone, near a hospital heliport, in a nature reserve or national park, or in a bird protection area with a seasonal ban. This is unknown until the location is fixed.
- EU rule change (Opinion 06/2026: 100 g threshold, operator identity checks, geo-zone framework) may change requirements during the project, probably from about 2028.
- Corporate constraints: Hexagon may have internal policies on drone brands (for example, DJI), data security, insurance or a flight-operations manual. Not researched; background knowledge only.
- Tropical campaigns: foreigners are often barred from registering drones (Indonesia, Brazil without a CPF). Expect multi-agency approvals (aviation, mapping or survey, defence, environment), customs and radio-homologation hurdles, and long lead times. Researching only in-country partners may be the only legal route.
- GDPR exposure from identifiable people in the Insta360 or drone footage published in the browser viewer. A face and plate blurring step is needed in the pipeline.

## FACT-CHECK: missed developments

- You cannot apply for a dissemination permit in advance. You can apply only with the finished material.: Lantmäteriet FAQ: 'Du kan inte heller söka spridningstillstånd i förväg', because protected objects change and each case needs a total-defence assessment. This settles the report's open question. Plan consequence: the order is capture, local processing, final model, application with the model and a map of the area, about 65 working days (roughly 13 weeks) of waiting, then publication. Each newly trained version, or a re-export that changes content, will probably need a new application (inference). Since 3D models cannot be retouched, edit the model yourself before applying, for example by cropping out a road or power line. (https://www.lantmateriet.se/sv/spridningstillstand/vanliga-fragor/)
- Elevated capture from a mast or pole with ground contact needs no Lantmäteriet permit and falls under no aviation rules.: The FAQ says images from high points with ground contact (mountains, buildings, cranes) need no permit; only luftfartyg trigger §9. A telescopic mast with the Insta360 (a few metres up) is therefore a cheap, permit-free way to get oblique views from above under the canopy. Drones (tethered ones too, background knowledge) remain aircraft. The drone-vs-ground comparison should consider this as an alternative. (https://www.lantmateriet.se/sv/spridningstillstand/vanliga-fragor/ https://lagen.nu/2016:319)
- Drone work flights for Hexagon need mandatory third-party liability insurance. The report treated this as optional.: Transportstyrelsen exempts only drones under 20 kg used for leisure, display or recreation. Other operations fall under Regulation 785/2004, with a minimum of 0.75 M SDR per accident for MTOM under 500 kg. Hexagon must confirm its cover before the first drone flight. (https://www.transportstyrelsen.se/sv/luftfart/luftfartyg-och-luftvardighet/dronare/dronarflygguiden/ https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32004R0785)

Under §30a, unlawful depiction is punished with fines or up to 1 year in prison. Guards may intervene against drones near such sites (§11). Add a skyddsobjekt check (look for signs; substations and military land) to the site checklist for every source module, not just the drone. (https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/skyddslag-2010305_sfs-2010-305/)

The project lives under C:\Users\...\Desktop\Code, which Windows 11 often syncs to OneDrive. Keep raw drone data and drone-derived models in a non-synced local folder. The project is not affected, but the date in the report is wrong. (https://lagen.nu/2018:1200 https://www.svenskforfattningssamling.se/doc/2025220.html)

## FACT-CHECK: recommendation assessment

The recommendations hold up. All of the load-bearing legal claims were confirmed against primary sources: §9 of lag 2016:319 is unchanged (SFS 2026:1378 changed only §6), a permit is needed for drone-derived 3D models, ground capture is outside the law, the cloud and email definition of dissemination, the free permit with about 65 working days of handling, no retouching of 3D models, and the narrow forest exemption. Keeping phase 1 ground-only and choosing option (c), a separate aerial layer, over (b) for anything public is well supported by the law.

Changes I would make:
1. Lantmäteriet cannot pre-approve. Applications are accepted only with the finished material, so the permit is a gate after processing. Plan about 13 weeks between the final drone-derived model and publication, and expect to reapply for each substantively new model version (inference).
2. Insurance is mandatory for Hexagon work flights under 785/2004, according to Transportstyrelsen, not just something to check.
3. Fix the camera surveillance date: the permit was removed by SFS 2025:220 on 1 Apr 2025. The project is not affected.
4. The 'keep data local' rule must explicitly exclude OneDrive-synced folders (the project sits under Desktop), Git remotes, LFS and CDNs, because Lantmäteriet names 'molntjänster' as dissemination.
5. Add a skyddsobjekt check (skyddslag §7 ban on depicting and measuring) for all sources, ground capture included. Option (a) is therefore not 'GDPR only'.
6. Track lineage, not just a flag, in the source-module interface. Any model trained on drone pixels inherits the drone's clearance status. A ground-only splat whose poses were only co-registered using drone SfM is a grey zone: treat it as uncleared or ask Lantmäteriet.
7. Consider a permit-free mast or pole elevated 360 capture (ground contact, so outside §9 and aviation rules) as a cheaper middle option before committing to a drone.

The drone-class advice (C0 or C1 in A1 to avoid the undefined 'recreational area' rule in A3; buy a class-labelled drone with Remote ID because of Opinion 06/2026) stands. Note that the Mini 5 Pro's C0 status depends on its certified configuration.

Several items could not be checked against primary sources: the tropical details for Brazil, Indonesia and Costa Rica, the landowner-consent provision, the bird-season dates and the 23 Oct 2025 start date of the supervised A2 exam. Keep these at low or medium confidence.

The web search budget was used up before this check began, so it relied only on direct fetches of known primary URLs. A follow-up search could look for pending §9 reforms not shown on Lantmäteriet's pages and for Hexagon's own drone policy.
